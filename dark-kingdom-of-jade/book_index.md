# Wraith: The Oblivion — The Dark Kingdom of Jade — Index

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index of *Wraith: The Oblivion — The Dark Kingdom of Jade* (White Wolf, 1995; written by Richard Dakan and Markleford Freidman, developed by Richard Dansky; 138 PDF pages). For exact quotes or precise facts, query `book_chunks.db` (see `README.md` in this folder) rather than relying on the summaries below.

**Page numbers.** Citations in this file are **printed page numbers** (the numbers on the pages and in the book's own table of contents) unless marked "PDF p.". **PDF page = printed page + 2** (printed p. 1 = PDF p. 3). In the database, `page` is the PDF page and `printed_page` is the printed one; use `query_chunks.py --printed N` to look up a printed page.

**Edition & crossover notes.**
- **Earlier edition; WR20 is the core.** This book was published in 1995 for the original-era *Wraith: The Oblivion* line. The core of this library is *Wraith: The Oblivion — 20th Anniversary Edition* (WR20, 2018), a rewritten, self-contained edition (`../wraith-20th-anniversary/`). Both use the same ten-sided dice pools (difficulty 6 default), so mechanics port easily, but **expect minor inconsistencies**, and where the two disagree **WR20 wins**. Typical differences: (1) WR20 splits every Arcanos dot into a Common and an Initiate art gated by Guild Status, while this book's Jade Arcanoi have a free *Basic Ability* plus five powers and no Guilds (WR20 Ch. 13 pp. 458–462 reprints the five-power ladders); (2) this book says the **p'o is the Shadow** played with the standard Shadow rules (p. 20), while WR20 p. 433 says Jade wraiths would "no sooner manifest a Shadow" even though its own Way of the Soul treats the p'o like one; (3) older numbers (e.g., fixed difficulties for Catharsis-type effects, the Living Family Pathos rules) versus WR20's Passion/Memoriam/Catharsis rolls; (4) small timeline differences (here Qin is sole sovereign of the Chinese dead within a year; WR20 says "less than a decade" for the eastern Shadowlands); (5) this book's "present" is the mid-1990s. WR20 Ch. 13 (pp. 433–441) gives a short, updated Jade overview that **agrees with this book on the main facts** (Qin Shihuang/Yu Huang, Immortal Guard of three regiments of 6,000, Chains of the Emperor, Lung Wang and the Ju-i, Xi'an and the Jade Palace in the Tempest, the four departments, White and True Jade, Ti Yu, Mrs. Meng, kuei and kuei-go). See `../library_index.md` for the full comparison.
- **Crossover for a *Kindred of the East* chronicle.** This book can also be used as crossover material for a chronicle of *Kindred of the East* (the Eastern-vampire line of *Vampire: The Masquerade*, which is **not part of this library**; neither Wraith book mentions it, so check details against your own copy). What this book offers such a chronicle: the **hun/p'o** model of the soul (pp. 19–20), **hungry ghosts and the Festival of the Hungry Ghosts** (pp. 25, 110), a bureaucratic **Hell** of magistrates, torturers and smelters (Ch. 6, pp. 87–103), **ancestor worship, Hell-note sacrifices, funerals, mediums, exorcists, talismans and feng shui** (Ch. 7), the regional Shadowlands and **Conquered Territories** (Ch. 5), a ready-made ghost empire with an unkillable guard (**Immortal Guard**, Ch. 3) and rebel movements (Ch. 4). `../library_index.md` has suggested hooks and cautions.

## Overview
*The Dark Kingdom of Jade* is the first "Dark Kingdoms" sourcebook for Wraith: a complete guide to the Chinese Shadowlands (the "Yellow Springs") and the empire of **Qin Shihuang, the Jade Emperor (Yu Huang)**, who conquered China in life and its afterlife in death. It explains the Chinese model of the soul (hun and p'o), the four-branch bureaucracy and the invincible **Immortal Guard**, the family-centred wraith society and its rebels, the slave-harvested **Conquered Territories** (Japan, Korea, Tibet, Southeast Asia), a walk-through of the Buddhist-style **Hell of Ti Yu**, the living Chinese practices that feed or banish the dead, and new rules (Martial Arts, Backgrounds, and the Ways of the Scholar, Artisan, Farmer, Merchant and Soul plus the Guard's Chains of the Emperor). Its recurring tension is stability vs. oppression: Chinese wraiths are spared harvesting only because foreign souls are enslaved in their place.

## Front matter, Ghost Story, Introduction (printed pp. 1–17; PDF pp. 3–19)

**Ghost Story: A Prelude to Hell (printed pp. 7–13).** First-person fiction by a wraith, Hshuin Wai Lei ("the Laughing Girl"), sentenced for disobeying her family and "treachery against the Empire" to wander **the Maze** — a gladiatorial maze of whirling White Jade blade-"Trees" in Hell, where wagering crowds watch captives be hunted by **kuei-go** ("Bad Dogs", jade-muzzled hound-wraiths). She frees a kuei-go by cutting off its jade muzzle (the muzzle is what makes its eyes green and its mind savage), survives, and an Imperial entourage grants her the right to "exist" and keep what she carried out; she bribes the kuei-go trainer to take the freed dog with her. On the road out of Hell she meets a motherly old woman offering tea beside a courier with empty black eyes (a foreshadowing of Mrs. Meng, Ch. 6).

**Introduction (pp. 15–17).** Premise: one man, **Qin Shihuang (Yu Huang, the Jade Emperor)**, the bastard son of a merchant who united China and then repeated the feat in the world of the dead, rules the Chinese afterlife for ~2,000 years; the Jade Kingdom covers the Shadowlands of China, Japan, Korea and much of Southeast Asia, is "the most aggressively expansionist" kingdom of the dead, and now "looks farther afield" (i.e., toward Stygia and the West). Rumors differ: terror state vs. enlightened monarchy. The book expands the *Zhongguo* section of the **Wraith Players' Guide** and is "the first Dark Kingdoms sourcebook" (© 1995). Intended uses: non-Stygian campaigns, non-Western characters, a Chinese wraith in a Stygian game, a mission to rescue a soul from Hell, hunting "rebels and hungry ghosts" in Tibet. Chapter-by-chapter overview on p. 16. Recommended reading/viewing/listening list (p. 17: *Tao Te Ching*, *Bridge of Birds*, Judge Dee, *Big Trouble in Little China*, Kurosawa, John Woo…). Short **Lexicon** (p. 17): Buddhism, Confucianism, Conquered Territories, Feng Shui, Feng-Tu, hun, Immortal Guard, Jade Palace, kuei, kuei-go, P'eng Lai, p'o, Qin Shihuang, Taoism, Ti Yu, White Jade, Yellow Springs, Yu, Yu Huang, Zhongguo.

## Chapter One: The Chinese Dead (printed pp. 19–25; PDF pp. 21–27)

**Summary:** Explains the Chinese model of the soul and why the Jade Kingdom differs from Stygia. Every person has two souls, the **hun** (mental/spiritual, created at conception) and the **p'o** (physical, created at birth); they separate at death and a wraith exists only when they *fail* to separate (premature death, or a family holding the spirit back with sacrifice and prayer). In a wraith the hun plays the **Psyche** and the p'o the **Shadow** — a wraith's p'o is played exactly as a Stygian Shadow, with the same rules, archetypes and conventions (p. 20). Dead souls go to one of three destinations: **Paradise (P'eng Lai)**, **Oblivion** (viewed here as natural, cyclical rebirth rather than annihilation — though many fear it anyway) or **the afterlife (the Jade Kingdom)**. The Emperor's afterlife is "a dark parody of Chinese beliefs and customs": souls are sent to Hell by his laws and whims, not by moral judgment. The chapter then covers the three "resources" of the Empire: jade, Eunuchs and kuei.

**Key themes:** family/ancestor worship as the foundation of wraith society (wraith families run by the oldest member regardless of gender; the living supply Pathos via sacrifices); Confucian hierarchy, face, obligation (Does it please the family? the law? will I lose face?); authority vs. oppression (Chinese wraiths live under a corrupt but stable bureaucracy; the **Conquered Territories** are slave camps harvested for souls to pay for that stability); the quest for enlightenment/P'eng Lai vs. duty to family and Empire; an ancient prophecy that the Emperor falls at the **fifth challenge** to his reign (Imperial histories deny any challenge ever occurred).

**Key terms/entities:** hun / p'o (pp. 19–20); P'eng Lai (p. 20); Yellow Springs; Conquered Territories (p. 21); True Jade vs. White Jade (p. 23); Eunuch, Abacus (p. 24); kuei (p. 25); Zhang Kunyi (p. 23).

**Notable rules/data points:**
- **True Jade** = jade buried with a corpse arrives in the Shadowlands as a relic; the most valued substance in the Kingdom (above souls). Stores Pathos usable by anyone holding it; jade weapons cause **aggravated** damage to Corpus; a True Jade blade cuts White Jade on Strength + Melee, **difficulty 4**; White Jade hews True Jade only at **difficulty 8**, and a botch shatters the White Jade blade (p. 23). Jade on the eyes/mouth keeps the p'o in the body (no kuei) but also makes a corpse more likely to produce a wraith; jade elixirs do the same.
- **White Jade** = souls smelted down; similar to Stygian soul-steel but inferior.
- **Eunuchs** = souls drained of will and used as living filing cabinets; made with **Moliate + Way of the Scholar**; invented by Protectorate minister **Zhang Kunyi**; white-jade collar; levers in the chest; tampering with an Imperial Eunuch is punished by taking its place. Each Branch has its own type (Jade Censors' "Abacus", Judges' law-codes, Army messengers, Protectors' confession-recorders) (pp. 23–25).
- **Kuei (hungry ghosts)** = the p'o alone; destroyed by sunlight; last ~3 years; Arcanoi never above level 1; one Fetter (their corpse); Pathos only from fear/hatred/despair; hunt in packs; the Militia must respond; can be imprisoned by jade on the corpse's eyes/tongue, or destroyed by three hits of blood/saliva. Sample kuei stat block on p. 25.

## Chapter Two: The History of the Jade Kingdom (printed pp. 27–47; PDF pp. 29–49)

**Summary:** A framed history ("Dear Jacob" — a letter from "Edward G." to a Stygian contact, compiled from Imperial records, Stygian battle diaries and confessions; p. 27) of how Qin Shihuang built the Jade Empire, from prehistory to the modern day. Before him the Chinese Shadowlands were clan-based petty kingdoms plagued by kuei and periodic Spectre incursions. In life the First Emperor (221 BC) burned books, buried scholars and grew obsessed with immortality; the mage **Fu Xia** (leader of the "Ways of the Spirits") promised eternal rule over the dead instead, and prepared his death: a necropolis modelled on his palace and a terra-cotta army of 24,000 soldiers, whose souls Fu Xia bound into the statues at a poisoned banquet (only 18,000 rose; the missing 6,000 made the Emperor suspect betrayal). Qin Shihuang woke in his tomb, cut the Cauls of his dead servants, raised the army, took the field and in under a year was sole sovereign of the Chinese dead — nearly losing to rebel leader **Li K'un**, who bargained with the Malfean **Lung Wang** (a white dragon) to destroy the Immortal Guard. The Emperor used Argos to fetch the **Ju-i** (jade scepter made by Fu Xia) and beat Lung Wang, later defeating/bending the Malfean in the Tempest and wringing its secrets from it; he returned changed ("all that was human was gone from him"), proclaimed himself **Yu Huang, the Jade Emperor**, and founded the **Jade Palace** in the Tempest, linked to the Yellow Springs by the **Great Imperial Highway** (finished c. 3rd century AD). Afterwards: soul-tax (25% of arriving souls go to the Palace), black markets, the **First Great Revolt** (Shandong enslaved by the Guard), the decision to feed the Empire with foreign souls (**Conquered Territories**: Mongolia, Tibet, Korea, SE Asia, finally Japan), the Buddhist **Second Great Revolt** (One Eyed Teacher; crushed at Xi'an → origin of **Feng-tu/Ti Yu**, Hell), the **Third Great Revolt** (assassins with Wu Fang Choi's soul-feeding device and the Dagger of Jing Ke reach the Palace and fail), and the modern era: war with Stygia, Japan's liberation and re-conquest, Hiroshima Maelstrom, the Communist families, and Imperial designs on the West.

**Key themes:** the Emperor as a possibly Malfean-tainted or Malfean-replaced ruler (official myth: he mastered Chaos; resistance version: Lung Wang consumed him and wears his face — "only the being wearing the Emperor's robes knows the truth", p. 34; a "demon" with a lizard-like head emerges from his bisected Corpus in 1953, p. 45); the Empire runs on foreign souls so that Chinese families can be spared harvesting; cyclic revolt and repression; religion (Taoist/Legalist state vs. Buddhist rebels vs. Confucian elites vs. Communist families).

**Key terms/entities:** Fu Xia, Ju-i, Lung Wang, Li K'un (pp. 29–32); Yu Huang, Jade Palace, Great Imperial Highway (pp. 33–34); soul tax; First/Second/Third Great Revolts and a "Fourth Great Revolt" (pp. 35–40, 46); One Eyed Teacher, Sima Qian, Apina (Indonesian mage), Wu Fang Choi, Dagger of Jing Ke (pp. 37–40); Feng-tu / Ti Yu (p. 39); Bugis ghost-fleets, General Peng Xin, Boston raid, Ivory Queen, New Orleans (pp. 41–42); Treaty with Stygia (p. 42); Hiroshima / Nagasaki Maelstrom, General Wu, Oda Nobunaga (pp. 43–45); Communist families, Cultural Revolution (pp. 46–47); "Thousand Years of Peace" (p. 46).

**Notable claims/data points:**
- **Stygia–Jade war and treaty (pp. 41–43):** Stygian outposts in the Jade Kingdom were destroyed; Charon's Skeletal Legion raids; Chinese wraiths in Stygia (railroad workers, Gold Rush) were rounded up into camps; Peng Xin's fleet, hired Bugis ghost-pirates and a rift in the Tempest hit Boston (accounts conflict). **Final treaty:** no raids/incursions, permanent embassies, each Kingdom has the right to souls of its own ancestry — all Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Tibetan, Vietnamese, Thai, Cambodian and Laotian souls belong to the Jade Emperor wherever they die; Stygia claims all Western souls. The Ivory Queen also signed; other Dark Kingdoms did not. A Midnight Express stop was built in Hong Kong; letter on p. 43 shows Stygian Legions still handing Asian souls over (via Gdansk).
- **World War II (pp. 43–44):** Japanese dead organized with Stygian help (1944), freed Japan in weeks, destroyed a small Immortal Guard unit at Kyoto; General Wu's re-conquest fleet was annihilated at Hiroshima — a **Great Maelstrom**; the Emperor devoured Wu in a cloud of darkness. Maelstrom damaged the Highway and Xi'an.
- **Korea (p. 45):** rebels' "National Dragons" (Moliated soldiers fused into giant beasts); Korea re-incorporated after the Korean War (final push 1953).
- **1953–62 (p. 45):** the Emperor himself, with the Ju-i and Guard, retook Japan; Oda Nobunaga's single-combat gambit; the Emperor's Corpus split and a white beast emerged.
- **Modern (pp. 46–47):** Communist-founded "false families" now hold magistracies in Shanghai, Beijing, Nanjing; the Emperor lets traditional vs. Communist families litigate each other to paralysis; since "the disappearance of Charon" the Protectorate and Army infiltrate Western cities and bribe Hierarchy members — "no plans for an invasion as yet."
- Sample "Tallies" memo (p. 36) quantifies souls/relics/losses taken from Korea, Mongolia and Nippon.

## Chapter Three: The Government (printed pp. 49–61; PDF pp. 51–63)

**Summary:** How the Jade Empire is actually run. Qin Shihuang, a Legalist in life (Han Feizi's seven principles of rulership are quoted on p. 49 — punish failure severely, reward success, issue unfathomable orders, "speak in opposites and act in contraries"), once governed personally but now rules from the Jade Palace through a bureaucracy that employs over one-third of the population. Government is **four equal, independent, mutually competing Branches**, each represented at every administrative level: the **Protectors of the Prosperous Realm** (loyalty/sedition), the **Judges of the Dead** (law enforcement), the **Imperial Army/Military** (defence) and the **Jade Censors** (census of souls and relics, reaping policy). Units: village → town → district/city → commandery → 34 provinces (each with a Governor, "mouthpiece of the Emperor") → the Imperial Court. The system is openly corrupt: laws are too numerous to publish (paper made from souls is dear), so officials memorise them or consult Eunuchs and often bluff; families use office to enrich themselves and trade favors.

**Key themes:** corruption as the working order; family-before-office; Legalist paranoia (the Emperor moves between 251 palaces by secret passage); each Branch checks the others; the Immortal Guard stands outside all law.

**Key terms/entities:** Four Branches / Four Magistrates (p. 50); Li Gao (Judge of the Dead, 300+ years in office), Fan Wushang (Protector since the 5th century), Hu Ji (Jade Censor, <100 years, huge personal wealth), Peng Xin (Commander of all the Armies) (pp. 52–53); Imperial Court, 251 palaces (p. 52); Caul, relic seal, citizenship lists (p. 53–54); Feng-tu / "Dread Mountain" (p. 56); Militia (p. 58); Immortal Guard, regiments, Chains of the Emperor (pp. 59–61).

**Notable rules/data points:**
- **Jade Censors (pp. 53–54):** every new wraith must be registered *before* its Caul is removed; one-month provisional membership in the reaping family while rival claims are heard; relic registration tax ≈ **5%** of appraised value (payable in relics/jade; seized after one month if unpaid); a thriving trade in forged seals; only Chinese-ancestry wraiths (plus recognised minorities) may be citizens — **Japanese and Europeans are barred** — unclaimed wraiths become Imperial property or are sent to Feng-tu; foreigners' souls go straight to Feng-tu. Sample City/District Censor stats p. 54.
- **Judges (pp. 54–57):** judge, jury, prosecutor and executioner; no police; civil vs. criminal cases (crimes against the state include treasonous plotting, forbidden Arcanoi, damaging Imperial property, lying to a magistrate, disobeying an Immortal Guard); punishments range from fines/forced service to Feng-tu ("once a wraith enters the Dread Mountain only the Emperor can save him"); appeals depend on bribes and favors. Sample Judge stats pp. 56–57.
- **Protectorate (pp. 57–58):** may go anywhere and demand any help; lying to a Protector is forbidden; they have **no enforcement power** — they bring evidence to a Judge/Censor/Military, and their word is treated as irrefutable; for sedition they summon the Militia or the Guard and there is usually no trial. Religion: personal Buddhism tolerated; **proselytizing, denying the Emperor's divinity and organizing pilgrimages out of the Kingdom are crimes**. Sample Protector stats p. 58.
- **Military (pp. 58–59):** any subject of any age or gender is liable to service; Commanders let families buy out service; weapons are restricted to soldiers; sample Militia Commander stats p. 59.
- **Immortal Guard (pp. 59–61):** terra-cotta skin hard as rock yet flexible, glowing unnatural reds/oranges; three regiments originally 6,000 each (current strength a secret, no replacements known); frozen in original rank (promotion only by destroying a superior); jade weapons; Argos, Leap of Rage, a private **Moliate** that works on other wraiths and themselves but cannot be Moliated by others; they cannot gain Pathos from the living — they must be **fed souls** (several hundred Pathos each, usable only by the recipient), and only the Emperor at Xi'an can perform the feeding (one known exception: the Third Great Revolt). Stat blocks for Footsoldier/Archer (Str 5, Corpus 12, Armor 5, Pathos max 100), Unit Commander (Pathos 150) and Regimental Commander (Pathos 200); only **18 Regional Commanders** command 1,000+ each (p. 61). They are the empire's enforcers by reputation in China and by terror in the Conquered Territories.

## Chapter Four: Life Among the Dead (printed pp. 63–71; PDF pp. 65–73)

**Summary:** Daily existence of a Chinese wraith, organized around the family. Traditional families (avg. 150–200 wraiths, often 20+ branches, led by the oldest member or a council) welcome a new wraith (a senior relative removes the Caul, a grace period of days to a year), assign Arcanoi training, and compete through lawsuits, marriage alliances and magistracies; they diversify (holding offices in all Four Branches vs. specialising), and depend on souls/relics from the living's sacrifices, kuei-harvesting, bribes and postings in the Conquered Territories. Outsiders can be adopted (with Censor approval) but are treated as servants. **Communist families** (invented families built by dead revolutionaries from the Fourth Revolt, legitimised by bribed Censors) are council-run, open to anyone, expansionist, switch loyalties easily, draw Pathos from the living democracy movement, and are tolerated — and infiltrated — by the Emperor as a counterweight to the great clans. For the individual: P'eng Lai-seekers, monks, Taoist sages; **"New Buddhism"** (the only officially sanctioned enlightenment philosophy: being a wraith is the last incarnation before Nirvana; the Shaolin Temple Haunt in the Songshan Mountains is the largest sanctioned temple); the state cult around the Emperor (no temples to Yu Huang; Confucian relations reduced to three: elder/younger, friend/friend, ruler/subordinate); and the **resistance**: **the Struggle Against the Qin** (open, armed; claims the Emperor is only a man) and the **Society of a Thousand Eyes and Hands** (infiltrates magistracies; halved after joining the Fourth Revolt prematurely). The chapter ends with **Spirits of Healing**.

**Key terms/entities:** traditional family, matriarch/patriarch, Festival of the Hungry Ghosts (p. 64); the Four Paths (Ways) (p. 65); Communist families (pp. 66–67); New Buddhism, Shaolin Temple Haunt (p. 69); state religion & Confucian relationships (p. 69); the Struggle Against the Qin, Society of a Thousand Eyes and Hands (pp. 70–71); Spirits of Healing, Sacred Trees (p. 71).

**Notable rules/data points:**
- **Spirits of Healing (p. 71):** a village group of wraiths with a shared **Memoriam pool (3–5)**; the pool rating = dice rolled per week (difficulty 8) to heal villagers: 1 success = one Health Level, 2 = cure a fatal disease, 3+ = "genuine miracles"; botches inflict Health Levels of damage equal to botches; usable only from the local Haunt; if the group is called on after exhausting itself and fails, the town pool loses 1 Memoriam, regained only after six months of failure-free effort.

## Chapter Five: Unreal Estate (printed pp. 73–85; PDF pp. 75–87)

**Summary:** A gazetteer of the Jade Kingdom and its Conquered Territories. The **Jade Palace** sits on a mountainous island in the Tempest, reachable only by the **Great Imperial Highway** (30 yards wide, White Jade bricks, lined with soulfire lanterns that howl like a Dirge if a traveller stops; a day's march from Xi'an to the First Gate; 251 palaces inside, a market-courtyard, Seven Dragons Gate; the Storyteller Note says only the Emperor knows it all — add anything). Important **Necropoli**: Beijing, Hong Kong, Nanjing, Shanghai. **Haunts**: the Shaolin Monastery, the Great Wall. **Rivers**: the Yellow River ("China's Sorrow") and Yangtze ("China's Fortune"). Then each **Conquered Territory** in turn.

**Key places/entities:**
- **Beijing (p. 74):** split between Communist families (base: Forbidden City; control Militia and Censorship) and traditionalists (base: Summer Palace; control Judges and Protectors).
- **Hong Kong (p. 75):** held tightly since the Stygian war; one of the few **Midnight Express** stops in the Kingdom, watched by Immortal Guard; very high wraith population (strong ancestor rites); Buddhist priests draw Protectorate scrutiny.
- **Nanjing (pp. 75–76):** very old, peaceful, traditionalist; ruled almost as a sub-kingdom by the **Zhu clan** under **Tai Zu** (first Ming emperor, r. 1368–98).
- **Shanghai (p. 76):** every wraith must register with a Communist family; a hidden stronghold of **the Struggle Against the Qin**, which controls most of the local Protectorate; Provincial Judge **Hang Xu** is quietly investigating.
- **Shaolin Monastery (p. 77):** sanctioned pilgrimage Haunt; monks form their own "family"; Protectorate screens teachers. **Great Wall (p. 77):** Haunt built from the bodies of conscript labourers; 1,500 miles, 15–30 ft high; its Shadowlands counterpart is believed in as protection and its watchmen guard the Quick; looking out from its towers can show Mongolia, the Tempest or other Dark Kingdoms — Stygian troop build-up (Skeletal Legion) has been spotted, so Yu Huang is massing troops on the Wall.
- **Rivers (pp. 77–78):** Yellow River dikes are built of White Jade, patrolled against Spectres and rebels, local Maelstroms can burst them; the Yangtze is the empire's main communications route (few Spectres, river pirates).
- **Conquered Territories (p. 79):** the Empire's economy and the Chinese families' freedom from enslavement depend on foreign souls; the governing Censor **auctions reaping rights** by region (highest bid in souls/yr wins; any surplus is kept). "A Note on Race": citizenship covers 40+ ethnic groups within the Kingdom's historic borders plus Hong Kong, Taiwan, Macao and Chinese in Singapore; everyone else is enslavable.
- **Japan (pp. 79–80):** the most troublesome territory; Necropoli are armed camps; rebels = **Fist of Nippon** (remnant of the Provisional Government), led by young **Kitagawa Hideo**, who has taught **Inhabit** (almost unknown in China) so wraiths slip into cities through power cables and telephone lines. **Hiroshima & Nagasaki (p. 80)**: still infested with Spectres from the atomic nihils; the Fist may hold part of Nagasaki. Sample Fist operative stats p. 80.
- **Korea (pp. 80–81):** **Yinbang** sympathiser families (citizens who help the Reapers; hated); resistance crushed in the last Great Revolt; **National Dragons** (10+ wraiths Moliated into one dragon and mind-linked by a Way of the Scholar variant — Str 9, Dex 4, Sta 8, Corpus 55, Pathos 25; p. 81).
- **Southeast Asia (p. 82):** client states (Vietnam, Cambodia, Laos, Thailand) paying tribute in souls/relics; hotbeds of spies. **Bangkok (p. 82)** is the closest thing to a free port, with ties to Swar. **Vietnam: the Lost Legion (p. 83):** Americans/Europeans from the Korean and Vietnam wars drafted into the Imperial Army 1950–75, treated as second-class, now a jungle resistance under **Colonel Roth** with Christian/Buddhist overtones.
- **Tibet (pp. 83–84):** Bon-Buddhist faith shapes the Shadowlands; every Tibetan wraith starts with ≥1 Memoriam; shrines store prayer-Pathos for defence of their site against non-Tibetan invaders, but **Moliate the wraith into the local deity and bind it to the shrine's boundaries** (leave it and lose the powers); few Tibetans become wraiths so resistance never broke the yoke; a dozen Immortal Guard have been sent to Lhasa.
- **The Bugis (p. 85):** Sulawesi pirate fleets (origin of "boogey man"), ghost ships that navigate the Tempest, trade with Spectres, hired by the Emperor but not trusted.

## Chapter Six: A Guided Tour of Hell (printed pp. 87–103; PDF pp. 89–105)

**Summary:** The Buddhist-flavored Hell that Yu Huang built, **Ti Yu ("Earth Prison")**, entered through the mountain at **Feng-tu** and located in the Tempest. History: the Buddhists brought Hell to China (128 hot hells, 8 cold, 84,000 others); a provincial governor named **Kuo** claimed in a stumbling, half-hallucinated expedition to have found the entrance at Feng-tu; belief thinned the Shroud there (effective Shroud **4**), and Yu Huang later tortured hundreds of Buddhist wraiths to learn what they feared, then built the real Hell on that template from White Jade smelted from Second-Great-Revolt rebels. Hell's purpose is to be a **factory of White Jade** (and a deterrent via released storytellers). The chapter walks a condemned wraith through the whole process: sentence → retention center → the **seven-day journey** → the trial → torture/smelting, then profiles the denizens.

**Key themes:** Hell as bureaucratic industry and propaganda; inescapable psychological torture ("designed to break the will"); the one flicker of hope — **"All the jailers take bribes"** (graffiti that appears whenever a prisoner vanishes) and Mrs. Meng's rest house; even the cruel are recruited from prisoners and live in terror of being demoted to prisoner.

**Key terms/entities:** Feng-tu / Dread Mountain, Ti Yu, Kuo (pp. 87–88); torc (inhibits Arcanoi like Stygian manacles) (p. 90); Weigh Bridge/Scales of Goodness, Bad Dogs Village, Mirror of Retribution, Inevitable River (pp. 91–92); **Mrs. Meng** and **Kao Chien-Li** (pp. 92–94); Hall of Justice, **Yen-lo Wang** (Prince of the Wheel of Law), **Ti-tsang Wang** (King of the Earth's Womb, ruler of Hell), Ministers **Wu-ch'ang Kuei** (Ghosts of Impermanence), **Ma Mien** (Horse Face) and **Niu T'ou** (Ox Head), Wheel of Law (pp. 94–101); the hells: **Nine Abysses, Hill of Knives, Sword Trees, Upside Down Hell, Bed of Fire, Jade Saw, k'ang** (pp. 95–98); torturers Three Finger Demon, Plaguebearer, Iron Mask (p. 101).

**Notable rules/data points:**
- **The journey (pp. 90–92):** prisoners are herded to Feng-tu twice a moon; Judges handle the movement; once past a certain point in the Labyrinthine caves a prisoner **loses contact with all Fetters**, and a Harrowing escape snaps back to near where the Corpus was lost, not at a Fetter — so intentional-Harrowing escapes are impossible. Seven days (Buddhist "seven weeks" compressed): (1) tax-collector "bandits" loot you; (2) rigged Scales; (3) Bad Dogs Village — kuei-go fight (sample kuei-go: Str 3, Dex 4, Sta 3, Corpus 15, Willpower 8 with muzzle on / 2 off, aggravated bites); (4) Mirror of Retribution = a minor Harrowing; (5) solitary confinement, time distortion; (6) cross the Inevitable River on a rope (**Dexterity + Athletics, difficulty 7, four rolls without a botch**; a botch drops you among serpents — sample serpent Str 7, Corpus 35); (7) Yen-lo Wang's realm. A Storyteller Note (p. 91) says the seven trials are setting, not required play.
- **Mrs. Meng & Kao Chien-Li (pp. 92–94):** a timeless rest house on the road. Mrs. Meng is **not a wraith** but a goddess-like protector who predates Qin; she forced the Emperor to let every prisoner have her hospitality. Kao Chien-Li (blinded lutenist, would-be assassin) escorts you. If you are courteous throughout (drink the tea, praise the lute, no violence, sleep through the night, don't wander) she offers Hell notes to bribe jailers — and if you politely refuse the bribe, Kao leads you to a fork: **left = Paradise, right = back to a Fetter with the torc removed.** Failing the test delivers you to a guard post. Sleeping there is a Slumber roll at difficulty 6.
- **Trial & sentence (p. 94):** Yen-lo Wang's verdict is predestined; most are sentenced to "material service" (torture until the soul can be forged), then thrown on the **Wheel of Law** (eight exits determine what object the White Jade becomes) and ground in a gristmill by Horse Face and Ox Head. It is rare to last more than a month in Ti Yu (p. 95).
- **Showpiece hells (pp. 95–97):** Nine Abysses (beams over a bottomless pit, kuei-go and archers; those who fall may reach the Void), Hill of Knives (race up a rising hill of blades; a quarter-day), Sword Trees (maze of True Jade blade-trees that pivot; five runners, kuei-go chase; winners sometimes recruited). **Efficient hells (pp. 97–98):** Upside Down Hell, Bed of Fire (holds 10; rarely survive >2 hrs), Jade Saw (heart-density reflects filial piety), boiling oil, impalement.
- **Release (pp. 98–99):** mild sentence = the k'ang pillory for a moon; skilled storytellers are sent in on purpose to spread terror; some are adopted by demons, recruited, or lobotomised/Moliated into muzzled kuei-go (confined to Hell, unlike barghests).
- **NPC templates:** Torturer (Attributes 9/5/3, Abilities 13/11/7, Arcanoi 7, Pathos 8, Corpus 10, Willpower 7; p. 102), Kuei-go Trainer / Keeper (10/6/5, 15/11/7, Arcanoi 8, Pathos 7, Corpus 9, Willpower 8; keepers live in Bad Dogs Village or the Keeper's Quarter, led by a Master Keeper and four Lesser Masters; p. 103), Smelter (9/7/6, 15/11/7, Arcanoi 10 with 5 in Way of the Artisan, Pathos 5, Corpus 8, Willpower 5; Overseers per four smelters; p. 103).

## Chapter Seven: The Quick (printed pp. 105–121; PDF pp. 107–123)

**Summary:** How the living of the Chinese world feed, contact, constrain and expel the dead — presented with rules hooks. Ancestor worship (family tablets, *Pai Chia T'ang*, "Saluting the Family Hall") ties wraiths to their families more tightly than ordinary Passions; sacrifices (Hell notes, paper houses/cars, buried grave goods that become relics) are the main source of Pathos; funerals decide whether a death makes a wraith or a kuei; festivals weaken the Shroud; mediums let families speak with wraiths; exorcists, talismans and feng shui keep unwanted ghosts out; fortune-telling is a Talent used by the living and the dead.

**Key terms/entities:** ancestral tablets, Pai Chia T'ang (p. 105); Living Family Background & "Family Welfare" Passion (p. 106); shrine Fetter (p. 107); Hell notes / spirit money, paper miniatures (pp. 107–108); Qingming / Clear Brightness (pp. 109–110), Feast of the Hungry Ghosts (p. 110), the Kindly Moon / All Souls Day, Society of Neglected Bones, "Censor's Portion" (pp. 110–111); spirit marriages (pp. 111–112); clairvoyantes and street mediums (pp. 112–113); exorcists (pp. 113–115); talismans (pp. 115–116); fortune-telling specialties (pp. 116–121); Chang Tao-ling, the first Master of Heaven (pp. 114–115).

**Notable rules/data points:**
- **Family Passion (p. 106):** anyone taking the Living Family Background must take **Family Welfare** Passion at ≥ the Background's dots; Pathos from family is gained at **difficulty 6**, from everyone else at **difficulty 8**; severe family trouble can force a Family-Passion roll (difficulty 6–9) for 1 permanent Angst, and a botch grants Pathos that *must* be spent helping the family or becomes permanent Angst.
- **Shrine Fetter (p. 107):** if the family keeps a shrine, it is a Fetter rated at the Family Background; any ghostly family member can Slumber there; ardent prayer can summon a wraith like a level-4 Argos *Jump*.
- **Sacrifices (pp. 107–108):** a bundle of Hell notes = 1 Pathos; suit of clothes 1, car 3, mansion 5 (if the wraith is present). If absent, the paper object appears and can later be cashed in via **Way of the Merchant**. Buried items become relics; technology works only if Pathos-fuelled. Censors take Hell notes for tax; they are the preferred bribe.
- **Funeral roll (pp. 108–109):** Perception + Etiquette, **difficulty 7** — success: hun and p'o separate (no wraith); failure: a wraith is created; botch: a **kuei**.
- **Festivals (pp. 109–111):** "Festivals for the Dead" weaken the Shroud and give extra Memoriam/sacrifices; modern public graveyards mean young wraiths arrive with less Memoriam and more become kuei.
- **Mediums (pp. 112–113):** clairvoyante = **Charisma + Empathy (difficulty 6)** to talk through a Fetter; street mediums let any wraith with Puppetry use *Master's Voice* at **−3 difficulty**, and cannot be conditioned into Consorts.
- **Exorcism (pp. 113–115):** an extended Willpower contest — banished when successes ≥ the target's Willpower; botches remove successes; Perception + Empathy (diff. 7) reveals a Fetter/Passion for +1 die each; wraiths spend Pathos (+1 difficulty each) or use Pandemonium; a **failed exorcism gives the ghost 2 Pathos, 3 temporary Angst and a 2-point Vengeance Passion**, and kuei follow the exorcist home. Cats can see and attack wraiths.
- **Talismans (pp. 115–116):** Hu Hsin Ching mirror (12 exist; Willpower diff. 8 or flee), Blade of Coin (resolve/destroy a Fetter; diff. 9; useless vs kuei), Willow Sword (strikes as True Jade, single use), Five Poison Tincture (wards kuei), Dragon-Tiger Elixir (see hidden wraiths; diff. 5), Jade Earring (Warded children; +2 difficulty to Skinride adults).
- **Fortune-telling (pp. 116–121):** a Talent (1 "Grandma taught you a few tricks" … 5 "Yu Huang has a question for you"); one specialty each: **Calendar** (lucky day −1 difficulty to related rolls, unlucky +1, a blunder that hurts a dead relative +2), **Astrology** (horoscope couplets), **Earthly Branches / Zodiacs** (12 animals with lucky/unlucky pairings, pp. 118–119), **Physiognomy** (14 animal types), **I Ching** (64 hexagrams), **Feng Shui** (house mirrors force a hungry ghost to Willpower diff. 8 or flee, a wraith diff. 6; practitioner rolls Perception + Fortune-Telling, difficulty 4–8, to set wards; Wits + Fortune-Telling diff. 7 to judge a site). Sidebar "Unheeded Warnings" (p. 121): the triangular Bank of China tower in Hong Kong has opened near-permanent nihils.

## Chapter Eight: Systems and Stories (printed pp. 123–136; PDF pp. 125–138)

**Summary:** The game-mechanical appendix for Jade Kingdom characters: new Merits and Flaws, Martial Arts as a replacement for Brawl, new Backgrounds, six new or replacement Arcanoi (the **Four Ways of Confucius**, **Way of the Soul**, **Chains of the Emperor**), and chronicle ideas. Character creation otherwise follows *Wraith: The Oblivion* (same points and costs; the p'o is created like a Shadow).

**Key rules/data points:**
- **Merits & Flaws (pp. 123–124):** Flaws — Dysfunctional Family (2), Unregistered Relics (1–3), Kuei Attractor (4; kuei within 100 paces are drawn to you), Antagonistic Family (2–4), Superstitious (1–3), Debt of Honor (1–5). Merits — Spirit of Healing (3), Eunuch (3), Old Age (2; −2 difficulty on Social rolls with your family), Favors (1–5).
- **Character-creation notes (p. 124):** Living Family and Wraith Family are the most common Backgrounds (a wraith with neither "is unlikely to survive for long"); Martial Arts is more common than Brawl; Inhabit is extremely rare; Taint of Corruption and Death's Sigil Thorns are rare outside Hell; most Fetters involve the living family.
- **Martial Arts (pp. 125–126):** replaces Brawl; choose a hard style (Karate, Shaolin Kung Fu, Tae Kwon Do, Wushu) or soft style (Jujitsu, Shuai-Chiao, Taijiquan, Aikido). Maneuver difficulties — Soft: Strike 6, Kick 7, Grapple 5, Throw 6; Hard: Strike 5, Kick 6, Grapple 6, Throw 7. Throw = Dex + Martial Arts, damage dice = Strength, stuns on 3+ levels. Each dot after the first buys a special maneuver — Soft: Counter Throw, Joint Lock, Deflecting Block; Hard: Elbow Strike (diff. 5, Str+1), Spinning Kick (7, Str+2), Blow to Pressure Point (8, Str+3), Damaging Block.
- **Backgrounds (pp. 126–127):** **Living Family** (replaces Memoriam for most; ladder from occasional prayers to daily prayers/sacrifices), **Wraith Family** (3–5 members … hundreds), **Magisterial Office** (assistant → commandery responsibilities).
- **Arcanoi (pp. 127–135):** all Stygian Arcanoi are known **except Castigate** (replaced by the Way of the Soul); Inhabit nearly unknown; Usury practiced secretly. Each new Arcanos has a Basic Ability plus five levels:
  - **Way of the Scholar** (hun; pp. 127–128): Studious Nature (Knowledge rolls −1 difficulty) · Discern the Lie · Inquiry · Word of Yu Huang · Filial Duty (3 Pathos + Willpower) · The Emperor's Servant (a day-long implant; how Eunuchs are made).
  - **Way of the Artisan** (jade/relics; pp. 128–130): Hear Jade's Tale · Shape True Jade · Work White Jade (3× material) · Rework Relics · Empower Jade (store Pathos/Arcanoi in jade) · Create White Jade (10 successes over 3 days, ~3 lb per Corpus level; a botch can create a kuei).
  - **Way of the Farmer** (p'o/kuei; pp. 130–131): Kuei Howl · Finding the Hidden Ghost · Cowing the Bestial Spirit · Purify · Deaden Spirit · Reap the Hungry Ghost.
  - **Way of the Merchant** (Pathos economy; pp. 131–132): Jingle Jingle · Transfer · Cash Money (convert sacrifices) · Withdraw Essence · Returning the Favor (give Pathos to the living) · Mint Money.
  - **Way of the Soul** (Taoist inner work; pp. 132–134): Negotiation (Willpower diff. 9 to calm your p'o) · Coax · Nullify Thorns · Give Higher Passions · Alter Demeanor · Inner Peace.
  - **Chains of the Emperor** (Immortal Guard; pp. 134–135): Soul Anchor (detect Argos within 50 ft) · Brilliance · Shackles · Snare · Lariat · Net (3 Pathos; 12-ft web; only True Jade cuts it).
- **Chronicle ideas (pp. 135–136):** Family vs Family / Rebels / Government; Imperiled Living Relatives; Family at the Imperial Court; Magistrates (vs Rebels, vs Magistrates, the Business of Governing, at Court); "Innocents Abroad" (Emissaries, Fugitives, Explorers, Mercenaries from Stygia); Rebels ("Cry Freedom!", "Viva la Revolution", "A Bat Out of…").

## Cross-cutting themes

- **Family vs. state.** The family is the core institution of both life and death (Ch. 1, 3, 4, 7, 8): it supplies Pathos, status and protection, and demands loyalty above all; the Bureaucracy is how families gain influence, and officials serve their family before their office. Mechanically this surfaces as the **Living Family / Wraith Family** Backgrounds, the **Family Welfare** Passion (Pathos at difficulty 6 from kin, 8 from others), shrine Fetters and the Spirit of Healing/Eunuch/Favors Merits (Ch. 7–8). Stories: Ch. 8 "Family versus…" ideas.
- **Souls as currency and the cost of stability.** Chinese wraiths are spared enslavement only because **foreign souls** are harvested (Conquered Territories, Ch. 1, 2, 3, 5); Reaping rights are auctioned to families (p. 79); White Jade is made in Hell (Ch. 6); 25% soul-tax and the Shandong enslavement show what happens when the supply fails (Ch. 2). Expect the Emperor to protect this system at any cost.
- **The Emperor's nature.** Fu Xia's necromancy, the Ju-i, the Lung Wang battle and the "something changed" passage (Ch. 2, pp. 31–35) vs. the resistance claim that a Malfean wears his face; the creature that burst from his Corpus in 1953 (p. 45); his withdrawal from daily rule behind 251 palaces (Ch. 3, 5); the prophecy that he falls at the **fifth challenge** (p. 22). Leave this ambiguous or resolve it as the chronicle needs.
- **Religion appropriated.** Legalist method of government (Ch. 3), Taoist divine title (Yu Huang), Confucian hierarchy (reduced to three relationships, Ch. 4), a Buddhist Hell built to punish Buddhists (Ch. 2, 6), "New Buddhism" as the only sanctioned enlightenment (Ch. 4). P'eng Lai (Paradise) is the stated goal but few families care.
- **Resistance as a cycle.** Great Revolts (Ch. 2), the Struggle Against the Qin and the Society of a Thousand Eyes and Hands (Ch. 4), Fist of Nippon, Korean Dragons, the Lost Legion, Tibetan shrine-guardians (Ch. 5), Mrs. Meng and Kao Chien-Li (Ch. 6). Nearly every rebellion is undone by infiltration, factionalism or the Immortal Guard.
- **Jade as material, magic and law.** True Jade (from burials) vs. White Jade (smelted souls) with exact cutting difficulties (p. 23); Artisans (Ch. 8), the Guard's weapons, the kuei-go muzzle, the Ju-i, Hu Hsin Ching mirrors, the Jade Earring, and jade on a corpse deciding whether you become a wraith, a kuei or neither (Ch. 1, 7).
- **p'o and hun.** The p'o plays the Shadow and the hun the Psyche (p. 20); the Way of the Soul replaces Castigate; kuei are free-roaming p'o; the Way of the Farmer tames them (Ch. 1, 8).
- **Stygia and the West.** War and treaty over souls (pp. 41–43), Hong Kong and the Midnight Express, Westerners drafted as the Lost Legion, Stygian spies and a Skeletal Legion build-up on the Great Wall (pp. 75–77, 83), and "since the disappearance of Charon" Imperial infiltration of the West (p. 47) — all useful hooks for crossover with Stygian chronicles (Ch. 8 "Innocents Abroad").
