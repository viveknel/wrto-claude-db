## Overview
*Wraith: The Oblivion — 20th Anniversary Edition* (White Wolf / Onyx Path, 2018; 594 PDF pages) is a complete, self-contained rebuild of the classic game of playing ghosts. Characters are **wraiths** with **unfinished business** ("Passions" and "Fetters" tie them to the living world), and each is shadowed by a **Shadow**, the dark half of the soul that wants to drag them into **Oblivion**. The setting is the Underworld: the Shadowlands behind the **Shroud**, the storm-torn **Tempest**, the Spectre-haunted **Labyrinth**, and the Void beyond. Its heart is **Stygia**, the **Dark Kingdom of Iron**, the empire of **Charon** (who vanished in 1945), run by a decaying **Hierarchy** of masked **Deathlords** and **Legions**, riddled with banned **Guilds** that hold the **Arcanoi**, the supernatural arts. The rules use ten-sided dice pools (difficulty 6 by default). The book is arranged as **Book One** (Introduction, Setting, Rules, Character, Traits, The Shadow), **Book Two** (Storytelling, Systems, Drama) and **Book Three** (Spectres, The Risen, The World of Darkness, The Other Dark Kingdoms) plus an **Orpheus** crossover appendix. Chapter 13 includes the book's own, shorter treatment of the **Dark Kingdom of Jade**, which overlaps with *Dark Kingdom of Jade*.

## Prologue: The Face of Death (roman pp. i–lxiv; PDF pp. 2–65)

A wordless-ish art-and-fiction prologue: short captions over full-page art follow a newly dead character (named "Kirk" on p. lx) as a Ferryman/Reaper ("the Incarnation of Death") guides them through death — the Harvesting, a Hierarchy courthouse where Thralls are judged, Drones, Heretics ("the Born Again"), a Necropolis, the Tempest, the Labyrinth's Spectres and the awakening of the Shadow — with essay-like passages on how death is a mask ("The mask of Death is whatever we make it to be", p. xxix), the denial of death, why wraiths remain (p. xlix), and why the Void and one's own Shadow must be faced ("I do not go gently", p. lxi). It contains no rules. Its use is tone and imagery; many pages are art with little or no extractable text (PDF pp. 17–26, 48, 63 are textless).

## Chapter 1: Introduction (printed pp. 15–23; PDF pp. 80–88)

**Summary:** Pitch and orientation. Wraith is "another way to tell ghost stories" in which players are ghosts with unfinished business ("you play the ghosts"); there is no winning or losing, success is "telling a satisfying story". This is the **20th Anniversary** version: after two decades of editions the classic metaplot "ended", and this book is a self-contained refresh — new players need nothing older, veterans will find streamlined rules and a more cohesive setting (p. 16). Describes the roles of Storyteller, Player (including **Shadowguiding**: each player also plays another character's Shadow), Character; tabletop vs. live-action play; the **Core Rules of Play** safety rules (no touching, know when to stop, no weapons, respect non-players; p. 19). Introduces what a wraith is and the Underworld in miniature, wraith characteristics (insubstantiality, Deathsight, Lifesight, sharpened senses), the Shadow, Oblivion, Transcendence, and the dual themes **"the Passion and the Horror"**. Ends with a ~100-entry **Lexicon** (pp. 21–23) and recommended reading/viewing.

**Key themes:** passion vs. horror; unfinished business and Fetters; the Shadow as inner enemy; Transcendence as the counterweight to Oblivion; storytelling-first design.

**Key terms/entities:** Quick, Restless, Skinlands, Shadowlands, Shroud, Tempest, Labyrinth, Void (pp. 19–20); Fetters, Passions, Pathos, Angst, Corpus, Psyche, Shadow, Thorns, Fronds (pp. 21–23); Stygia, Hierarchy, Legions, Deathlords, Dictum Mortuum, Necropolis, Citadel; Charon (vanished 1945 fighting the Spectre Gorool in the **Fifth Great Maelstrom**; Onyx Tower abandoned); Guilds ("13 Greater and three Lesser"), Arcanoi; Risen, Doomslayer, Helldiver, Renegades, Heretics (all p. 22–23).

**Notable claims/data points:**
- **Wraith characteristics (p. 20):** Insubstantiality; **Deathsight** (see decay/weak points); **Lifesight** (auras of the living); sharpened senses (and sensory overload).
- **Great Maelstroms:** "There have been five so far in the history of Stygia" (p. 22). Charon "vanished in 1945" (p. 21).
- Dice: ten-sided dice (p. 18). 13 chapters plus the Orpheus appendix are listed on p. 17.
- Chapter-title naming: the book's TOC calls Ch. 4 "Character" and Ch. 5 "Traits"; the overview on p. 17 calls them "Character Creation" and "Traits and Guilds"; Ch. 13 is "The Other Dark Kingdoms" in the TOC but "The Dark Kingdoms" on p. 17.

## Chapter 2: Setting (printed pp. 25–93; PDF pp. 90–158)

*(Part 1 of 3 — Geography, metaphysics and the history of Stygia. Later parts cover the Economy/Metaphysics sections, the city of Stygia and the Guilds.)*

**Summary of geography/metaphysics (pp. 25–27):** The world of Wraith is a gothic-punk version of ours. **Skinlands** = the living world; the **Shroud** (a membrane of fear and disbelief that arose after the **Sundering**) separates it from the **Shadowlands**; the **Fog** is the living's mental defence against noticing ghosts (children and animals, especially cats, are largely immune). The **Underworld** is an "onion": Shadowlands → the **Tempest** (eternal storm with islands of stability — Stygia and the other Dark Kingdoms — and the **Far Shores**) → the **Labyrinth** (Spectres, Neverborn) → the **Mouth of the Void / Oblivion**. Oblivion once was passive entropy; the Sundering woke it, birthed the **Malfeans**, who gnawed out the Labyrinth, and gave the **Shadow** a voice in every wraith.

**History of the Dark Kingdom of Iron (pp. 27–41) — timeline:**
- **Before the Sundering** (p. 27): living and dead mingled; **the Lady of Fate** prophesies a leader. **Charon** (from Mycenae) is given a reed boat, explores the River of Death, finds the **Isle of Sorrows** (p. 28), and is acclaimed leader; the **Ferrymen** (Restless who followed him) swear to guide souls across the **Sunless Sea**; some became the **Shining Ones** who found the **Far Shores** (p. 29). **Payment for the dead** (coins on the eyes) originates with Charon's tithe (p. 28).
- **The First Descent** (p. 29): Charon enters the Labyrinth via the **Veinous Stair** and returns with the smith **Nhudri**, who invents **soulforging** (forge **Kyklos**; **oboli**; **Soulsteel**; p. 30).
- **Roman era — the Republic of Stygia** (pp. 30–32): Charon founds Stygia on seven hills with a lighthouse; a seven-seat Senate; "Lux Veritatis" (preserve humanity's art and thought); roads through the Tempest; the Equitaes (death-knight cavalry); the Fishers (Christian wraiths) leave for "Paradise".
- **First Great Maelstrom** (476 AD, fall of Rome; p. 32): destroys the Republic; Charon rebuilds as **emperor** over eight **Legions** led by masked **Deathlords** (seven plus the Lady of Fate), assigned by manner of death. The Ferrymen rebel and found **Dis** (p. 32–33). Order of the Sickle (p. 33).
- **Treaty of Paradise; Hierarchy; Guilds; Dictum Mortuum** (Crusades era, pp. 33–34): feudal Hierarchy (Thralls at the bottom); wraith **Guilds** form and later resent Charon; the **Dictum Mortuum** forbids contact with the living.
- **Second Great Maelstrom** (1347, Black Death) and the **Fishers' Revolt** → the **Great Evacuation**, the **Proclamation of Reason** (the Shining Ones are Heretics), the **Council of Inquisitors / Grim Riders**, **Stygia Besieged** (pp. 34–35).
- **Three Abominations** (16th c.; p. 36): *Looting of the Artifacts* (Spear of Longinus taken), *Breaking of the Guilds* (1598), *Flaying of Obsidian* (Heretics/Renegades skinriding Iberian crews annihilate the Dark Kingdom of Obsidian and Ix Chel). → **Third Great Maelstrom** (general Coldheart; Shroud thickens).
- **Industry & Necropoli** (pp. 37–38): Thralls mine death-ore on the Veinous Stair; first **Necropolis** in London, then Rome, Paris, New Amsterdam, Philadelphia, Boston; iron ships carry souls to Stygia.
- **The Fading / Second Descent** (pp. 38–39): Charon's Fetters are destroyed; he withdraws to the Onyx Tower; Deathlords squabble. **Great Influx** (WWI; Aug 1, 1914), **Fourth Great Maelstrom**; Spectres infest Necropoli and the living cities; the **Great Depression** (p. 39–40).
- **Charon Emerges** → founds the **Doomslayers** (bounty-hunters of Spectres) (p. 40); **World War II** (Holocaust victims, Pacific clashes with Jade's ships; p. 40); **Fifth Great Maelstrom** (1945, atom bombs) and the Malfean **Gorool**; Deathlords refuse to help; Charon's **Third Descent** (continues on p. 41).

*(Part 2 of 3 — Aftermath, economy, metaphysics, the city of Stygia, Necropoli. Continued below.)*

- **Aftermath (p. 41):** Charon's Third Descent — he sailed into Weeping Bay, fought Gorool, opened a whirlpool and vanished with it; the Deathlords vow "Stygia would continue" and went back to scheming. Theories (p. 41): Gorool was Charon's excised Shadow, or the Shadow of the whole Dark Kingdom of Iron.
- **Souls and Memories: the economics (pp. 42–45):** everything is made of *souls* (soulforged) or *memories* (relics of destroyed Skinlands things). Stygian currency is the **obolus** (one soul, slate-gray coin, can be cut into fragments — prices as low as 1/32 obolus — fragments re-fuse). **Soulforging** (heat Corpus over soulfire, hammer into **soulsteel**; one soul = one item; **Artificers'** monopoly, Nhudri still at his forge) vs. **Stygian steel** (Tempest gel-plasm + Veinous Stair ore; more malleable, self-repairing). **Relics** (ghosts of loved/broken Skinlands things; fade unless re-invested) vs. **Artifacts** (made in the Underworld; durable). Most trade is **barter/credit**: "every deal between two is between four" (always have a witness; your Shadows are watching). Sources of souls: Spectres, **Drones**, plasmics, criminals sentenced to the forge, and **Harvesters** who enslave Enfants (p. 42).
- **Metaphysics (pp. 46–50):** the **Caul** (an Enfant is bound to the Shadowlands for days equal to its strongest Fetter), **Corpus** (plasm; heals fast; Harrowing if destroyed), appearance idealised by self-image, **Reaping** (cutting Enfants from Cauls — some benign mentors, many Harvesters), **Thralls** (bound with **Reaper's Chains** that block Arcanoi and Harrowing escape), **Masks** (status symbols; Charon's mask; Deathlord masks), **Drones** (stayed too long in the Caul), **Deathmarks** (reveal Legion and Guild), **Psyche and Shadow** (Shadow has Angst and Thorns; wraith vs Spectre = who is winning), **Passions and Fetters** (Passions generate Pathos; Fetters anchor and heal; can change by *Resolution* or *Destruction*; see p. 305 for new Passions), **Catharsis** (Shadow takes control for seconds–hours) and **Harrowings** (dragged through a Nihil to a nightmare psychodrama; "Destruction Harrowings" end in the Void).
- **Stygia — the city (pp. 50–61):** approach by river or rail (Midnight Express; zeppelins ended by "four-bell" Maelstroms in the late 1970s); the Isle of Sorrows is sickle-shaped (8 miles tip to handle, 15 along the curve) and buried under layers of relic buildings. **Landmarks:** **Onyx Tower** (Charon's locked tower; lighthouse still lit), **Road of Lords** (ridge road linking the Citadels), **Road of Steel** (ring road of silver-white soulsteel), **Road of Souls** (east-west), **Sea of Souls** (viscous soulforged bay), the **Seawall** (100-ft soulsteel), **Iron Hills** (eight islands, ~500,000 wraiths in poor conditions), the **Emerald Keep**, Emerald-to-Veinous-Stair approach. **Citadels (north→south) and Legions:** Seat of Shadows (**Iron Legion**, Ashen Lady — died of age/time; district **Ironville**), Seat of Dust (**Skeletal**, Skeletal Lord — disease; **The Spine**; the lord believes he is Pestilence), Seat of Burning Waters (**Grim**, Smiling Lord — violence; **Hangtown**), Seat of Succor (**Penitent**, Laughing Lady — victims & perpetrators; **Sanctuary**), Seat of Silence (**Silent**, Quiet Lord — suicide/despair; **Newtown**), Seat of Golden Tears (**Legion of Paupers**, Beggar Lord — mysterious deaths; **South End**), Seat of Thorns (**Emerald Legion**, Emerald Lord — happenstance; **Whilestown**); the **Lady of Fate** (Legion of Fate) keeps her Citadel on the Isle of Eurydice, 20 miles offshore. Other districts: **Lower Bay**, **Upper Bay** (the Hermitage, Fallen Rome, Dominus Ex Anima), **Riverside** (the Library built over the Library of Alexandria, Fountain of Caroline), **Onyx Point**.
- **Necropoli (pp. 61–63+):** a Hierarchy outpost over a Skinlands city, with a Citadel, an **Anacreon** council and Legion garrisons. Notable: **Edinburgh** (model Necropolis; built on a volcano with a Nihil), **Los Angeles** (a Guild town — Masquers ascendant — star wraiths; oil-rig Nihils), **Paris** (underground in the catacombs; Anacreon Vauban of the Grim Legion).

*(Part 3 of 3 — Necropoli, factions, the Hierarchy, Legions and Guilds.)*

- **More Necropoli (pp. 64–66):** Philadelphia (fractured: Eastern State Penitentiary Citadel vs. Lucretia Mott's anti-Thralldom Fairmount Park Citadel; Quaker Heretics; yellow-fever Renegades), **Rome** (Grim Legion-ruled by Anacreon Agrippa; ancient wraiths with millennia-old Fetters; Vatican City is separate). **Ranks of civilians in a Necropolis (p. 65):** Chancellors, Ministers, Inspectors, Adjusters, Clerks.
- **Factions (pp. 65–68):** **Renegades** (political opponents of Stygia; gangs include Brotherhood of the Black Rabbit, Daughters of the Scythe, Doyle's Irregulars, Storm Riders, the Flying Column; rumored Renegade Council/"Renegade Lord"; Renegades and Spectres are not natural allies) and **Heretics** (religious dissidents since the Proclamation of Reason; cults include Riders of the Wheel, Children of the Green, the Host, Cult of Nhudri). **The Hierarchy** (p. 67–68): the bureaucracy that keeps Stygia running; corrupt at the top, idealistic locally; advancement often requires a superior's destruction.
- **Ferrymen & the Midnight Express (pp. 68–69):** outside Stygia; capital **Dis**; the **Severance** (the Ferryman's Shadow is excised and becomes an independent Spectre called a **Pasiphae**; immune to Catharsis); lantern, scythe, hooded robe; since the Fifth Great Maelstrom they brokered the **Covenant of the Millions**. The **Midnight Express** (engine #13, engineer J. W. Stillwell) arrives at each stop at midnight, runs free and keeps strict peace aboard.
- **Legions (pp. 70–85):** rank ladder — Legionnaire, Centurion, Marshal, Regent, Overlord, **Anacreon** (Anacreons of every Legion except Fate form each Necropolis council) (pp. 70–71). In brief: **Emerald** (happenstance; the four *Emerald Values*; Assessor States; Marcus Lowry's small-unit "Ranks", pp. 71–73) · **Skeletal/Gaunt** (pestilence; "Mister Bonyhands", *Journals of Bone*; longbows; pp. 73–74) · **Paupers** (mystery/ambiguous deaths; spy network; sub-Legions of the Onyx Tear and of Revelations; pp. 74–76) · **Fate** (those whose deaths mattered; Isle of Eurydice; Stygian steeds, Equitaes; pp. 76–78) · **Penitent** (madness; Seat of Succor; Keepers; Skirmisher/Phalanx/Beast/Flesh/Machine Corps; commander Liamh; pp. 78–80) · **Iron** (old age; Ashen Lady; Salon, MPPU, Mediators, Advocates; pp. 80–81) · **Grim** (violence; promise of confronting one's murderer; Great Ledger; Infiltrators/Retrievers/Executioners; pp. 81–84) · **Silent** (despair/suicide; "the only all-volunteer Legion"; Steel Martyrdom; hidden Solicitors and Mnemoi; the Ten Thousand; Rushes of Lethe; pp. 84–86).
- **The Guilds (pp. 86–93):** trade cartels/secret cabals that grew from Roman-era *collegia*; after the First Great Maelstrom Stygia offered monopolies (*litterae patentes*) → the **War of the Guilds**; **Council of Guilds** (1358/1405); the Solicitors were crushed; the **1598 Coup** led by Flux-using **Alchemists** (with Mnemoi manipulation) failed and Charon issued **The Breaking**. Today officially illegal, tolerated: **High Guilds** (Artificers, Pardoners, Masquers, Usurers), **Working Guilds** (Chanteurs, Harbingers, Oracles, Sandmen), **Criminal Guilds** (Spooks, Haunters, Proctors, Puppeteers, Monitors) and **Forbidden Guilds** (Solicitors, Mnemoi; membership = automatic soulforging). **13 of 16 Guilds** sign the revised **Compact of the Guilds** (p. 92). The Mnemoi (memory-manipulating ex-judges) are the most reviled; no one remembers what they did (p. 93).

**Key themes (Ch. 2):** souls as raw material and slavery as policy; a decaying empire with immortal, entrenched leaders; Legions as bureaucracy-by-death-cause; Guilds as both essential and forbidden; Stygia as a city assembled from the memory of lost buildings; "yes, and" advice to Storytellers about adding favourite lost architecture (p. 54).

**Key terms/entities:** Isle of Sorrows, Veinous Stair, Onyx Tower, Road of Lords/Steel/Souls, Iron Hills (pp. 51–61); Legions and Deathlords (pp. 70–85); Anacreon, Overlord, Regent, Marshal, Centurion (p. 70–71); Thralls, Reapers, Harvesters (pp. 42, 47); oboli (pp. 44–45); soulsteel, Stygian steel (pp. 42–45); Dictum Mortuum, Covenant of the Millions (pp. 34, 69); Renegades, Heretics, Ferrymen (pp. 65–69); Compact of the Guilds (p. 92).

## Chapter 3: Rules (printed pp. 95–103; PDF pp. 160–168)

**Summary:** The compact core rules of the Storyteller-style system. Rules are "intentionally abstract"; the **Golden Rule** is "If you don't like it, don't use it" (p. 95). **Time** is measured in turn → scene → downtime → chapter → story → chronicle (p. 96). Roll only when both success and failure are interesting (pp. 96–97).

**Core mechanic (pp. 97–100):**
- Dice are **ten-sided**. Declare an action → Storyteller picks a **difficulty (2–10; default 6)** and the relevant **Attribute + Ability** (or other two Traits) → player rolls a **dice pool** equal to the combined dots (Willpower/Corpus-type Traits are not usually combined; pools above 10 are very rare, p. 98).
- A die ≥ difficulty is a **success**; **a 10 is always a success**; **each 1 cancels one success**. Difficulty labels: 3 Easy, 4 Routine, 5 Straightforward, 6 Standard, 7 Challenging, 8 Difficult, 9 Extremely Difficult (p. 98).
- **Degrees of success (p. 99):** 1 marginal, 2 moderate, 3 complete, 4 exceptional, 5 phenomenal. **Failure** = no successes, or 1s ≥ successes. **Botch** = no successes *and* at least one 1 (p. 100). **Automatic success** if the dice pool ≥ difficulty (marginal success; p. 100).
- Trait ratings: x abysmal, • poor, •• average, ••• good, •••• exceptional, ••••• superb (p. 98). Traits are Attributes, Abilities (Talents/Skills/Knowledges) and Advantages (Backgrounds, Arcanoi, Passions, Fetters, Willpower, Pathos).

**Optional rules (pp. 100–101):** Botches; Multiple Actions and Split Dice Pools (use the smallest pool and divide it); Extended Actions (accumulate successes over turns); Resisted Actions (subtract opposing successes); Teamwork (add successes); Trying It Again (+1 difficulty for each previous failure, not for combat). A sidebar warns "Complications are complicating" — drop any rule that hurts play.

**Lexicon (pp. 101–103)** of gaming terms (Ability, Action, Advantages, Attributes, Backgrounds, Botch, Chapter, Chronicle, Demeanor, Dice Pool, Difficulty, Downtime, Extended Action, Knowledge, Nature, Rating, Resisted Action, Scene, Skill, Story, Storyteller, System, Talent, Trait, Turn, Willpower) and Wraith terms (Angst, Arcanos, Catharsis, Corpus, Dark Passions, Eidolon, Fetters, Passions, Pathos, Psyche, Shadow, Shadowguide, Thorns, Troupe).

**Compatibility note:** this is the same ten-sided, difficulty-6 pool system that *Dark Kingdom of Jade* assumes (its stat blocks, "difficulty 7", "Dexterity + Athletics" etc. read identically), so DKoJ mechanics port to WR20 with only the trait-name caveats listed in `library_index.md`.

## Chapter 4: Character (printed pp. 105–123; PDF pp. 170–188)

**Summary:** Step-by-step character creation. Because a wraith "has already had a life", creation starts from three questions — **Who were you? How did you die? Why are you still here?** (pp. 107–110) — with lists of concept, cause-of-death and unfinished-business seeds (Addict, Artist, Blank Slate, Cop, Crook … Workaholic; Accident, Elements, Illness, Mystery, Old Age, Overdose, Something Strange, Violence; Confusion, Failure, Guilt, Legacy, Love, Missed Chances, Mission Possible, Need to Understand, Resentment, Revenge, Unfulfilled Destiny, Unrevealed Truth). Advice for Storytellers on linking the Circle and running **Preludes** (short premortem/death/Reaping scenes; pp. 106–107, 120–121). **Setting Limits** and **Disability** sidebars (pp. 108, 116). A full worked example (Deputy "Ed", pp. 116–120) and a Q & A backstory prompt list (pp. 121–123).

**Creation procedure (chart, p. 111):**
1. **Concept** — choose concept, **Nature** and **Demeanor** (Archetype list on pp. 110–111: Activist, Architect, Avant-Garde, Bon Vivant, Bravo, Bureaucrat, Caregiver, Child, Competitor, Conniver, Critic, Deviant, Enigma, Explorer, Fanatic, Follower, Gambler, Jester, Leader, Martyr, Mediator, Optimist, Penitent, Pragmatist, Rebel, Rogue, Scientist, Survivor, Traditionalist, Visionary).
2. **Attributes** — each starts at 1 dot; prioritize Physical/Social/Mental as **primary 7 / secondary 5 / tertiary 3** extra dots (Strength, Dexterity, Stamina; Charisma, Manipulation, Appearance; Perception, Intelligence, Wits).
3. **Abilities** — Talents/Skills/Knowledges prioritized **13 / 9 / 5**; no Ability above 3 at creation.
4. **Advantages** — **Arcanoi 5 dots** (max 3 in any one at creation; split into Common vs Initiate powers; Guild membership via the Guild Background), **Backgrounds 7**, **Passions 10** (statement + core emotion + rating 1–5), **Fetters 10** (rating 1–5).
5. **Finishing touches** — **Corpus 10** (cannot be raised with freebies); **Pathos = 5 + Memoriam** (max 10); **Willpower 5** (max 10); **15 freebie points**.
6. **Shadow creation** — Chapter 6 (a player can add up to 7 freebie points to their own Shadow's pool in exchange for extra personal freebies, or reduce it point for point; p. 114).

**Freebie costs (p. 114):** Attributes 5/dot, Arcanoi 5/dot, Willpower 2/dot, Abilities 2/dot, Passions 2/dot, Backgrounds 1/dot, Fetters 1/dot, Pathos 1 per 2 dots, Corpus cannot be increased.

**Key rules/data points:**
- **Pathos uses (pp. 114–115):** spend 1 to change direction in the Tempest; spend 1 to regain 2 Corpus (normal damage, 1 Pathos per turn); Slumber/meditate 8 hrs + 3 Pathos to regain 1 aggravated Corpus level; once per session roll Memoriam (diff. 8) for Pathos; roll a Passion (diff. 6 if experiencing, 8 if watching others) for Pathos.
- **Willpower uses (p. 115):** spend 1 per turn for an automatic success (announce before rolling); resist the Shadow by rolling permanent Willpower; fulfilling your Nature can regain Willpower; **losing all Willpower triggers a Harrowing**.
- **Fetters (p. 114):** allow Slumber and serve as lifelines out of a Harrowing; they decay if neglected; **a Fetter reduced to 0 triggers a Harrowing**. Fetters can be "given to the Storyteller" to keep them secret.
- **Arcanoi ↔ Guild table (p. 112):** Argos–Harbingers (travel); Castigate/"Castigation"–Pardoners (wrestling Shadows); Embody–Proctors (manifest); Fatalism–Oracles; Flux–Alchemists (decay and reconstruction); Inhabit–Artificers (machinery/computers); Intimation–Solicitors (desires); Keening–Chanteurs; Lifeweb–Monitors (Fetters); Mnemosynis–Mnemoi (memory); Moliate–Masquers (plasm); Outrage–Spooks; Pandemonium–Haunters; Phantasm–Sandmen (dreams); Puppetry–Puppeteers (possession); Usury–Usurers (vital energies).
- **Specialties:** see Ch. 5 (rating 4+; reroll 10s once). **Prelude** types and alternates are on pp. 120–121.

## Chapter 5: Traits (printed pp. 125–219; PDF pp. 190–284)

**Summary:** The full trait catalogue and the entire Arcanoi/Guild reference — the largest chapter (≈1,400 chunks). Sections: Specialties; Basic Abilities; Archetypes; Attributes; Abilities; Backgrounds; Arcanoi (rules) and then each of the 16 Guilds with its Arcanos and all ten arts.

**Rules/data points (general):**

## Chapter 6: The Shadow (printed pp. 221–249; PDF pp. 286–314)

**Summary:** The Shadow is the part of the Psyche that wants to drag the wraith to Oblivion — slowly, enjoying the fight (pp. 221–223). The chapter covers how another player **Shadowguides** (roleplays) each wraith's Shadow (pp. 223–227), how to create a Shadow (pp. 227–234, with an example), how **Angst** works (pp. 234–236), the **Catharsis** roll (p. 236), **alternate Shadowguiding methods** (pp. 236–238), an Example of Play (p. 239) and the full **Harrowing** rules (pp. 240–249).

**Shadowguiding (pp. 223–227):** a Shadowguide (1) speaks to the Psyche, (2) offers **Shadow Dice** (negotiated directly with the Psyche), (3) triggers **Thorns** (through the Storyteller), (4) runs **Harrowings**, and (5) may initiate **Catharsis**. Suggested share ≈ **20% of table time**; random draw or Storyteller assignment; respect limits (the Shadow may push a *character's* buttons, never the *player's*); sidebar "Best Dirty Tricks for Shadowguides" (p. 226).

**Shadow creation (pp. 227–233):**
1. **Archetype** (p. 227–230): The Delver, Abuser, Director, False Friend, Parent, Martyr, Monster, Somnambulist, Perfectionist, Voice of Hope, Stormcrow.
2. **Angst** — roll Willpower dice vs. difficulty 6 (1s don't cancel); successes = starting permanent Angst, at least 1, never above Willpower.
3. **Dark Passions** — 7 points (same format as Passions: action + core emotion + rating).
4. **Freebies** — 10 points for more Angst (temporary up to 9), Dark Passions (never more dots than the wraith's own Passions), or **Thorns**.
- **Thorns (costs):** Spectre Prestige, Dark Allies, Tainted Relic, Infamy (1/level); Death's Sigil (1–3); Shadow Traits, Nightmares, Aura of Corruption, Mirror Mirror, Shadow Call (2 each); Pact of Doom, Trick of the Light, Bad Luck, Shadowed Face (3); Honeyed Tongue, Tainted Touch (4); Shadowplay, Shadow Familiar, Freudian Slip, Shadow Life (5); Whispers, Manifestation (6); Devil's Dare, Vampiric Nature (7) (pp. 231–234).

**Angst (pp. 234–236):** the Shadow's fuel; **temporary Angst can exceed permanent (max 10)**; at 10 temporary it converts to +1 permanent; **at 10 permanent the Shadow takes over permanently and the wraith becomes a Spectre.** Sources: certain Arcanoi, accepted **Shadow Dice** (each 1 rolled = +1 temporary Angst; up to 5 dice offered), **Dark Passions** (roll vs. difficulty 7), Storyteller discretion. Losses: Shadow-dominated botches, **Castigate**, Storyteller reward. Angst triggers: acting against your code, helplessness, failing a vital task, indecision, losing a Fetter/Passion, disastrous decisions.

**Catharsis (p. 236):** the Shadow spends 1 temporary Angst and rolls its temporary Angst vs. the Psyche's **permanent Willpower + Eidolon dice**, both difficulty 6; the winner controls the wraith **for the following scene**. While dominant the Shadow can use the Psyche's Arcanoi (costing the Psyche's Pathos), can't spend Willpower and can't reduce Pathos below 1; Angst-gaining Arcanoi use *lowers* Angst; the Shadow may hide its actions (Eidolon roll diff. 7 to resist). When temporary Angst exceeds the wraith's Willpower the Shadow may attempt Catharsis.

**Alternate Shadow methods (pp. 236–238):** Dedicated Shadowguide, Storyteller Shadows (best for 2–3 players), Deterministic Shadowguiding (the Storyteller offers Shadow Dice), No Shadow (not recommended).

**Harrowings (pp. 240–249):** a nightmare psychodrama in the Labyrinth directed by the Shadow and a Spectre "Director". Triggers (Targeted): **losing/using all Willpower** (target a Passion or Fetter chosen by the Shadowguide; success also restores Willpower = Intelligence), **about to lose the last point of a Passion or Fetter**, **losing all temporary Corpus** (success returns Corpus = Stamina; failure costs 1 permanent Corpus). **Destruction Harrowings:** last remaining Passion, or **zero permanent Corpus** (roll permanent Willpower vs. Shadow's permanent Angst, difficulty 6; failure = destruction, or optionally a Nephwrack Spectre). **Self-inflicted Harrowing:** Willpower diff. 9, 2+ successes — always a Destruction Harrowing. **Group Harrowings** share a Fetter loss or Corpus loss. **Running one:** ~5 minutes to set up (Harrowed player leaves the room), ~10 minutes to roleplay; if unresolved the Storyteller calls a **permanent Willpower roll, difficulty 9** (single success). **Eidolon:** once per session roll Eidolon (difficulty 9) to alter an aspect of the Harrowing. Rewards: +1 to a defended Passion/Fetter, or −1 to −3 temporary Angst after a Destruction Harrowing; failure: −1 dot on the Quarry (and a roll for a second Passion at difficulty 5), or lost Fetter. Alternate systems: Storyteller-driven, Mechanistic, Solo, No Harrowing (p. 248–249). Two Examples of Play: a successful Harrowing (the Chagall windows dilemma) and a failed one (refusing help).

**Key terms/entities:** Psyche, Shadow, Shadowguide, Thorns, Angst, Dark Passions, Shadow Dice, Catharsis, Harrowing, Quarry, Director, Nephwrack, Eidolon, Spectre Prestige…Vampiric Nature (pp. 231–234).

**Compatibility with DKoJ:** DKoJ says the **p'o plays the Shadow** and the **hun the Psyche**, "the same rules, archetypes and conventions apply" (DKoJ p. 20); a Jade wraith's p'o is therefore built with WR20's Shadow-creation rules above. (WR20 p. 433 says Jade wraiths "would no sooner manifest a Shadow", but WR20's own Way of the Soul, pp. 461–462, uses Shadow Dice, Thorns and Angst on the p'o, so the mechanics agree.) The Way of the Soul powers Coax, Nullify Thorns, Give Higher Passions, Alter Demeanor and Inner Peace are already printed in WR20 terms; only DKoJ's basic "Negotiation" (a fixed-difficulty p'o Catharsis calm-down) uses older numbers. WR20's Catharsis roll is Angst vs. Willpower + Eidolon, difficulty 6. Mrs. Meng's rest-house and the Jade Hell's torc/Harrowing-escape rules (DKoJ pp. 90–93) assume Harrowings send you back to a Fetter; WR20 sends failures into the Tempest and successes to a Fetter, Haunt or nearby safe spot (p. 242).

## Chapter 7: Storytelling (printed pp. 253–277; PDF pp. 318–342) — *Book Two*

**Summary:** Pure Storyteller advice — no secret information. The Storyteller builds the world every session, adjudicates (rules "a starting point, not an absolute"; fudge freely but stay consistent, p. 254) and above all makes sure "everyone, including yourself, has a good time" (p. 254). Sidebars: *Play vs. Fun* (heavy themes are legitimate if everyone consents), *The Big Canvas* (any historical figure or lost object can appear since "everything dies", p. 255), *The Big Question* (yes, wraiths can have sex; pregnancy is impossible, p. 258).

**Key sections and ideas:**
- **Elements of Wraith (pp. 255–258):** Themes (Adventure, Defiance, Fear, Immortality, Inner Conflict, Intrigue, Mystery, Triumph) and Moods (Romantic, Ominous, Tragic, Mysterious, Exciting, Lighthearted); **The Big Three** — *fear* (anticipation), *terror* (demands action, allows hope), *horror* (violation and hopelessness) (p. 258).
- **Storytelling Tools (pp. 258–265):** **Events** (purpose, scope and scale, pacing), **Storyteller characters** (friends/allies must "provide something the Circle lacks"; antagonists need goals and distinctive voices; "It's Okay to Cheat Sometimes", p. 261), **Setting** (choose a base setting that supports the mood; Stygia = intrigue; a dying Rust-Belt Necropolis = Skinlands focus), **Staying Together** (shared Fetters, a shared enemy, Nihils dumping lost characters nearby), and **Conflict** types (Wraith vs. Hierarchy / Renegades / Heretics / Guild / Legion / Circle / Spectres / Quick / Other Supernaturals / Shadow / Self; pp. 264–266).
- **Chronicles (pp. 266–273):** plot-driven vs. character-driven; **chronicle concepts** — *Shadowlands:* Guardian Angels, Poltergeist Party, Dead Kids on the Block, Necropolis Mine, Reapers Keepers, Deadeye Detective, Let's Be Dead Cops, Heretic Shuffle, "Oh Mama I'm In Fear for My Life" (Renegades), Taking the Band on the Road, Chain Breakers; *Stygia:* The Great Game, Guild as Charged, The Summons, Stormcrow, New in Town, In Ranks; *The Tempest and Beyond:* Doomslaying, Chosen of the Ferrymen, Sailing the Sea of Shadows, **Ambassador to New Lands** (a Deathlord's emissary to another Dark Kingdom — a ready hook for Jade, p. 269), Seeking Transcendence, On the Dark Side (play Spectres), Early to Rise (become Risen). Story seeds: To the Walls, End of a Fetter, Mistaken Identity, The Great Escape, Take a Ride (Midnight Express), Quid Pro Quo (pp. 270).
- **Passions and Fetters as story engines (pp. 270–273):** write up to a page per Fetter; vary a Passion's core emotion ("Protect my lover" with Love vs. Greed vs. Pity); build stories *around* a Passion as theme.
- **Advanced Techniques (pp. 273–275):** flashbacks (run like a Harrowing), foreshadowing, dreams (Sandmen), symbolism, lost memories (Catharsis, Mnemoi), unreliable narrators, **crossover games** (vampires, Garou, Euthanatos mages and Sluagh fae naturally interact with the dead; the Storyteller decides what crossover is allowed; pp. 274–275).
- **Endings (pp. 275–277):** character destruction (tweak dice if death would be "goofy, annoying, or emotionally unsatisfying"; a destroyed player can return as a Spectre/Doppelganger), chronicle endings (timing, power, aftermath), and **Authority & Responsibility** (helping or removing struggling/disruptive players; p. 277).

**Compatibility with DKoJ:** DKoJ's "Chronicle Ideas" (Family vs. Family/Rebels/Government, Magistrates, Innocents Abroad, Rebels, DKoJ pp. 135–136) map directly onto WR20's "Ambassador to New Lands", "Chain Breakers", "Oh Mama I'm In Fear for My Life", "The Great Escape" and "Seeking Transcendence" — the two books' Storyteller advice is complementary, not conflicting.

## Chapter 8: Systems (printed pp. 279–311; PDF pp. 344–376)

**Summary:** Three sections — *Setting* (Reaping, the Shroud and Fog, Maelstroms, Haunts, Injury), *Perceptions* (Transcendence), *Character Development* (XP, Passions, Fetters, Slumber). It is the reference chapter for wraith-specific rules that are not Arcanoi.

**Key rules/data points:**
- **Reaping the Caul (pp. 280–282):** removing your own Caul = spend 1 Willpower, roll Willpower diff. 4, each success adds Pathos (can exceed 10 and makes you glow); **guided Reaping** (Reaper spends 1 Willpower; Charisma + Empathy vs. difficulty **9 − Memoriam**; Reaper gains Pathos = Memoriam + 2); **Soul Harvesting** (rip off the Caul; no mechanical cost). Optional **Crucible scenes** (play out the death/Caul transition).
- **The Shroud (pp. 282–284):** rating **4–10** by location (10 sunny shopping center … 4 abandoned cemetery at a new-moon midnight; table p. 284); lowered by 2 near any supernatural creature; **Hysteria** (frightened crowds lower it up to 3, never below 4); a **Haunt** lowers it by its rating; recordings of wraiths are blurred.
- **The Fog (pp. 284–285):** mortals' reactions by Willpower (1 catatonic … 10 no reaction; table p. 285). Children, animals and some supernaturals are exempt.
- **Maelstroms (pp. 286–288, 296):** rated in **Bells** (1–5; 6/continuous = Great Maelstrom); damage dice per turn: 1 bell 4, 2 bells 6, 3 bells 8, 4 bells 12, 5 bells 16 (p. 296); hazards: Howling Winds, Freezing Rain (drains Pathos), Isolating Fog, Searing Lightning.
- **Haunts (pp. 288–291):** reduce the Shroud by rating; **echoes** (Deadly, Forbidding, Isolated, Jinxed, Nightmarish, Obscured, Resonant, Slipping) fill any reduction below 4; **Anchoring** adds the Haunt's rating to time in the Shadowlands; **shelter** from Maelstroms (subtract the Haunt's rating from the Maelstrom level; max 5 wraiths per rating level).
- **Injury (pp. 291–297):** Corpus 10 permanent; states **Corporeal, Incorporeal, Wavering, Material, Harrowed** (p. 292; "Rules of Ouch and Excuse Me", p. 293); damage types **bashing /, lethal X, aggravated ★** (p. 293–294) — Stygian steel, **barrow-flame**, **soulfire**, Outrage and Usury arts, Spectre powers and supernatural claws cause aggravated; **soak = Stamina diff. 6**; healing: bashing 1 level/hour, 1 Pathos = 2 bashing or 1 lethal, aggravated = 3 Pathos + 8 hrs Slumber/meditation per level, **Slumber 8 hrs clears bashing and Stamina roll (diff. 6) heals lethal** (p. 295); falling damage table (p. 297).
- **Transcendence (pp. 297–300):** one-way, "no mechanical path"; **The Tomes of Golden Light** and the **Eight Truths of Transcendence** (practice patience; cultivate the higher self; conquer the Shadow; peace with yourself and with the living; identity over Corpus, Transcendence over identity; perfect the positive virtues); possession of the Tomes is "seditious materials" in the Empire; Storyteller advice on playing it (pp. 299–300).
- **Experience (pp. 301–305):** award **2–3 XP per session** (1 automatic, +1 Learning Curve, +1 Roleplaying, +1 Circle, +1 Heroism); **end-of-story** bonuses (Survival, Success, Danger, Wisdom, Shadow); the **Shadow gets ⅓ of the Psyche's XP (rounded up)**. **Costs (p. 302):** Attribute current×4; Ability current×2 (new Ability 3); Arcanos current×3 (new Arcanos 7); Willpower current rating; Passion/Fetter/Background by Storyteller permission (new Passion 3, improved Passion current×3; new/improved Fetter ×3 of target; Background suggested 4 to open, then ×3 current). **Mastering Arcanoi** with a Mentor or Guild (Background roll diff. 6/8 reduces cost; p. 303).
- **Passions (pp. 305–307):** roll Passion rating (diff. 6) once per scene for Pathos (diff. 8 for secondhand emotion); **Waning** (Neglect roll diff. 9, Harrowing, Numbing, Resolution); **Resolving a Passion** (weaken, then a Willpower roll vs. original rating + 4 needing successes = original rating; failure raises it by 1; botch = Destruction Harrowing).
- **Fetters (pp. 307–311):** Willpower regained by interacting with a Fetter (roll rating diff. 6; Slumber in one diff. 4); no Fetters = 1 Willpower per scene in the Shadowlands; **+1 difficulty in the Tempest, +2 in the Labyrinth** with unresolved Fetters; Neglect roll diff. 9; Rending (Spectre power); new Fetters ×3; **Resolving a Fetter** (halve first; roll Willpower vs. 4 + original rating; botch destroys the Fetter and triggers a Destruction Harrowing; resolved Fetters let a Fetter-less wraith stay 1 day per Willpower point). Optional **Ship of Theseus** rule.
- **Slumber (pp. 311):** meld into a Fetter ≥ 8 hours; a damaged Fetter ejects you (1 lethal Corpus); ancient wraiths sometimes sleep for decades; Onyx Tower pilgrims dream of Charon.

**Key terms/entities:** Caul, Reaping, Harvester, Crucible scene, Shroud rating, Hysteria, Fog, Bells, Haunt echoes, Wavering, Rule of Ouch, barrow-flame, soulfire, Tomes of Golden Light, Eight Truths, Resolution, Numb the Heart, Rend the Lifeweb (p. 308).

**Compatibility with DKoJ:** DKoJ's Living-Family Pathos rules (diff. 6 from kin) and its "Memoriam pool" healing by Spirits of Healing are *Jade-specific alternatives* to WR20's Passion-rolls (diff. 6) and Memoriam (diff. 8, once per session). DKoJ's Fetter-as-family-shrine and funeral roll (Perception + Etiquette diff. 7) are additive; nothing contradicts WR20's Haunt/Shroud rules, though DKoJ's Feng-tu "effective Shroud 4" is simply the WR20 minimum.

## Chapter 9: Drama (printed pp. 313–339; PDF pp. 378–404)

**Summary:** Structure and standard dramatic systems: time units (turn → scene → chapter → story → chronicle, plus downtime; pp. 313–315), how to run **action scenes** (describe the setting vividly; use turns only in action scenes), **Feats** (Automatic, Physical, Mental, Social) and **Combat**. "The more dice rolls you can avoid through roleplaying, the better" (p. 313).

**Key sections and numbers:**
- **Combat turn (pp. 315, 326–329):** three stages — **Initiative** (roll 1 die + Dexterity + Wits; declare actions in *reverse* order; delaying allowed), **Action** (attack **difficulty 6**: Dexterity + Brawl / Melee / Firearms / Athletics), **Resolution** (**each extra success adds one die to the damage pool**; damage rolls vs. difficulty 6, one Corpus level per success; soak = Stamina, reflexive, never below 1 damage die; only certain Moliate/Outrage arts and armor soak aggravated). Armor classes 1–5 (rating 1–5; penalty 0 to −3 on Dexterity pools; destroyed if one attack's damage ≥ 2× its rating; p. 328).

## Chapter 11: The Risen (printed pp. 385–403; PDF pp. 450–468) — *Book Three*

**Summary:** Rules and Storyteller advice for wraiths who cross back over the Shroud and reanimate their own corpses ("the Risen"), plus Risen character creation, the two Risen-only Arcanoi, and chronicle ideas. A Risen is a wraith who has fought back across the Shroud for a single **obsession**; the **Shadow** must agree to come along and rides in the **Conduit**. Becoming Risen should be "an earned privilege" and is mythic, dangerous and usually short. It is illegal under the Dictum Mortuum; the Hierarchy hunts Risen and those who teach the art.

**The Risen Commandments (pp. 386–387):** You have unfinished business · You are not a zombie · The Shadow rules (no Pact, no Rising) · Returning is forbidden · Getting to the other side is not easy · Staying on the other side is harder (muted senses, living folk sense something is "not right", Angst builds faster).

**The five steps of Rising (pp. 387–393):**
1. **Intent** (pp. 387–388): the "why" — an obsession reducible to one object plus one verb (protect, hide, destroy, inform…). The Shadow may twist it. *Shortcutting the Steps* sidebar (p. 387): **the Rabbi** (a mentor shortcuts the investigation), **the Artifact** (5-dot; stays behind in the Shadowlands; works on one wraith), **No Labyrinth** (negotiate directly). Only Intent and the Pact are sacrosanct.
2. **Investigation** (pp. 388–390): track down sources (rumor is "a game of telephone"), negotiate, then **train**: **Puppetry 1 + 2 dots in any mix of Lifeweb, Inhabit, Embody**. Four ways to handle the training time (as long as it takes / learn en route / montage / Circle shares it). Sidebar: **The Order of Acherontia Styx** — the Order of the Unlidded Eye's Risen-hunting arm; each member has Risen at least once (p. 389).
3. **The Pact** (pp. 390–392): the wraith travels bodily to the **Labyrinth** (via Harbingers' Argos, Helldivers, or following Spectres through the Tempest) and bargains with her Shadow, whose terms can be anything ("let me be in charge", "feed a Dark Passion", "destroy your favorite Artifact") — **the wraith invests 1 permanent Willpower** and both sides are bound; the wraith must fulfil the Shadow's terms in good faith or the Shadow gains 1 permanent Angst. Shadow motives: watch the Psyche fail or twist its reason for returning. Spectres may interrupt the negotiation.
4. **The Rising** (pp. 391–392): the body must be reasonably intact (not cremated or decapitated; a heart and brain remnant is needed; skeleton impossible). The stitching-back is agonizing: relive the death, tissue regrows, embalming fluids and worms are vomited up, then claw out of the coffin. **System:** spend **1 Pathos** and roll **Dexterity + Puppetry (diff. 6)**; then spend **1 Willpower** and roll **Willpower (diff. 8)**. Failing Puppetry = stay a ghost; failing Willpower = **Shadow takes the body, Psyche goes into the Conduit**; botched Willpower = Shadow in control + 1 permanent Angst per 1 beyond successes.
5. **Existence as Risen** (pp. 393–394): senses muted yet augmented (Deathsight, Lifesight, Heightened Senses), obsession-driven; "happy endings" are impossible; ending the stint — voluntary return (contested Willpower vs. temporary Angst, diff. 7), body destroyed, Conduit destroyed (Destruction Harrowing), or Passion resolved (drift back within 24 hours); Transcendence while Risen is theoretically possible but very unlikely.

**The Conduit (p. 392; sidebar *Animal Conduits*):** the wraith's strongest Fetter (small, portable) hosts the Shadow; separation = **+1 difficulty on all Willpower rolls**; the Conduit has **10 health levels** and damage to it hurts the Risen with no healing; destroying it = Destruction Harrowing. Living mortals and intangible things can't be Conduits; a family pet can (its psyche is obliterated, the Shadow gets a mobile body).

**Risen character creation (pp. 394–395):** Strength ≥ 3, Appearance ≤ 3; Arcanoi: Puppetry + 2 dots in Lifeweb/Inhabit/Embody, 2 spare dots (including Fascinate/Serendipity); **5 Backgrounds** (no Artifact, Mentor, Status); **10 Fetters** (primary = Conduit) and **10 Passions**; **Pathos 5, Willpower 6, 15 freebie points** (costs: Abilities 2, Arcanoi 5, Attributes 5, Backgrounds 1, **Disciplines 7**, Fetters 1, Passions 1, Pathos 1, Willpower 2). **Vampire Disciplines for Risen (sidebar p. 395):** **Celerity, Fortitude, Potence** only — 10 XP for first dot, then current ×5; Potence/Fortitude add dice equal to rating for 1 Pathos; Celerity gives extra actions. **Traits of the Risen (p. 395):** no sleep/breath/food (vomits when eating), feels neither pleasure nor pain except aggravated damage, warm(ish), scars show; Shadow created normally (Underworld-specific Thorns such as Tainted Relic don't work); wraith-to-Risen conversion rules and Storyteller option to swap lost Backgrounds/Arcanoi for Skinlands equivalents (pp. 395–396).

**Risen Arcanoi (pp. 396–398; no Guilds, single path each):**
- **Fascinate** (mind-influence): • Distraction · •• Remembrance (trigger-based instruction, up to a month) · ••• Charge of Duty (2 Pathos) · •••• Overriding Behavior (3 Pathos + 1 Willpower; OCD tic) · ••••• Target Lock (3 Pathos + 2 Willpower; mania — Storyteller should set limits and discuss with players beforehand).
- **Serendipity** (fate): • Fortuitous Wandering (Serendipity + Awareness, diff. 6) · •• Flashback (2 Pathos, diff. 9 − Fetter rating) · ••• The Face in the Crowd (2 Pathos, diff. 9 down to 5) · •••• Fortuitous Meeting (1 Willpower + 3 Pathos; target within 100 miles) · ••••• The Drum Beats On (4 Pathos + 1 Willpower; diff. 6/7/8/9 for 3 turns/scene/day/week; extra successes lower later rolls).

**Storytelling the Risen (pp. 399–403):**
- **Angst:** any Arcanos use other than Fascinate/Serendipity gives +1 extra Angst; difficulty on rolls to gain Angst is **−1** (p. 399).
- **Corpus/damage:** health = Corpus (max 10 equivalent); no pain penalties except aggravated (fire −1 die per wound level, min 2 dice); heal **1 Corpus per turn by spending Pathos** (the Shadow can do the same with Angst); at 0 Corpus but Pathos left = unconscious for Stamina + Fortitude (diff. 10 − Pathos): 5+ successes one turn · 4 one scene · 3 ten minutes · 2 one hour · 1 six hours · 0 twelve hours · botch a day+ (p. 400). Zero Corpus and Pathos = ejected, body returns to decay, stunned for turns = Shadow's Angst. Destroying heart **and** brain = Destruction Harrowing and no further Rising.
- **Catharsis as Risen (pp. 399–400):** triggered when temporary Angst exceeds permanent Willpower, **when temporary Angst converts to permanent Angst, or when the Risen burns all Pathos with Corpus left**; Psyche goes to Conduit (Shadow may cut her senses); Shadow can use Arcanoi but not Thorns; duration a scene (Angst-gain rolls) or until Pathos regained (12 hours' control; regain roll diff. 8 for driving Passion, 9 for other Passions); reverse Catharsis (Willpower vs. current Angst) once Pathos ≥ Angst.
- **Slumber (p. 400):** must touch a Fetter/Conduit; Stamina roll diff. 7 to heal aggravated damage. **Travelling between worlds (p. 400):** to return, 1 Willpower + 1 Pathos + Willpower roll diff. 7 (gains temporary Angst); to re-Rise, Dexterity + Puppetry diff. 5 + Willpower diff. 8 and the Shadow gets a second round of bargaining. Sidebar: *Alternate Destinations* (Rising in another body is rumor).
- **Conflicting issues (pp. 400–401):** solo vs. Circle; short vs. long term; hard for characters vs. fun for players (time-lapse the training); sidebar *Playing Well with Others* (Risen in crossover games).
- **Chronicle types (pp. 401–403):** Agents of the Other; Stopping the Rampage; Turning the Screws (the "Rising" was a Harrowing all along); Working for the Man (Stygian agents); Lone Gunman; Part of the Puzzle; **Caveat Emptor** — Shadow bargaining styles: Hooking the Customer, The Denier, Get Out of Dodge, Hidden Objectives, The Straightforward Deal, Appetite for Destruction.

**Key terms/entities:** Risen, Intent/obsession, Pact, Conduit, Fascinate, Serendipity, Rabbi, Acherontia Styx, Dictum Mortuum.

**Compatibility with DKoJ:** DKoJ (1995) has no Risen rules. If a Jade wraith is Risen, use WR20's Pact and Conduit rules with the **p'o** standing in as the Shadow and the **hun** as the Psyche (DKoJ p. 20); DKoJ's **kuei** are *not* Risen. For a Kindred of the East chronicle, a Risen wraith loose in the Skinlands is the natural way to bring a Jade-born ghost's unfinished business into a Kuei-jin story (see `library_index.md`).

### The Dark Kingdom of Jade (pp. 433–441, 458–462)
- **Hun and p'o (p. 434):** WR20 opens the chapter with "The dead of the Yellow Springs would no sooner manifest a Shadow than Stygian wraiths would emerge from their Cauls with the hun and p'o" (p. 433). The hun is "roughly analogous to the Psyche"; the p'o is baser, tied to the body, family and Fetters, and "cares not for Transcendence or Oblivion." Most Jade wraiths are horrified when their Cauls are removed (they are a *failed* separation). Options: P'eng Lai/Paradise by denying the p'o and Fetters, or live in the Shadowlands.
- **Imperial Map (pp. 434–435):** China, Mongolia border with Stygia in Siberia, Japan, Korea, SE Asia (Thailand in its sphere), spreading to Polynesia; rumors of Yu Huang targeting North America's western Necropoli. A little over **20 provinces**; main capital **Xi'an** (near Qin's tomb); at least 4 other capitals; governors and mayors; pagodas versus new high-rises and industrial parks (pp. 434–435).

## Appendix: Orpheus (printed pp. 468–490; PDF pp. 533–555)

**Summary:** A self-contained crossover appendix on the **Orpheus Group**, a modern Skinlands corporation whose **projectors** (living agents who separate their souls from their bodies) walk the Shadowlands. It supplies history, a lexicon, character-creation rules for projectors, **Shades** (personality templates), **crucibles**, **Stains**, **hues** (pigment-addict wraiths) and storytelling advice for Orpheus chronicles or mixed projector-and-wraith games. Includes a **spoiler alert** (p. 470): *Wraith: The Oblivion* originally ended in 1999 with *Ends of Empire* (a final catastrophic Maelstrom); *Orpheus* was a core book published four years later.

**What Orpheus is (pp. 469–470):** coded-website, word-of-mouth firm selling investigations of hauntings, contact with the dead, corporate espionage and (quietly, expensively) assassinations; recruits people with several **near-death experiences (NDEs)**; uses meditation, drugs and cryonics. Lexicon (pp. 469–470): *Beach, Black Heroin/Pigment, Cradles, Crucible, Fumigation, Gauze, Hue, Lament, Malkovich, Meat, NDE, Networking, Post-Life Entity, Project Echo, Projector, Skimmer, Sleeper, Spook, Squatter*.

**History (pp. 470–473, plot-heavy):** **JDG Cryogenics** (founded by Bob Jackson and Anne Del Greco; cryonics specialist **Dr. Eduardo Fernandez**) → client **Jane Kennedy** proves projection and funds the rebrand as **Orpheus** → botanist **Dr. Amours Katlilian** finds the addictive plant *Kakos stromithicarum* in the Bosawás reserve → **NSA "Project Flatline"** (twelve death-row inmates led by Uriah Bishop become wraiths and escape; cover-up fire at Marion Federal Penitentiary) → **Project Echo** (Orpheus's study of wraith society) → NSA's first projectors are slaughtered by Spectres ("Shadow Games"); the drug supply moves to **pigment**. Sidebar *Pigment* (p. 474): smoked, swallowed or injected effects, with dice penalties and Willpower halving.

**Project Echo's six activities (pp. 473–476):** recruitment, intelligence gathering, espionage, retrieval, removal ("fumigation": hunting Fetters), salvation; plus wetwork for the NSA.

**Rules ("Flatlining — Orpheus Systems", pp. 477–488):**
- **Projectors vs. wraiths:** no Shadow (they have **Stains**), **Vitality** instead of separate Pathos/Willpower/Corpus links (max Pathos ≤ temporary Corpus; spend 1 Willpower for 3 Pathos), **Tethers** instead of Fetters (strongest = silver cord to the body, rating 5; not resolvable; self-repair), no Caul; Dead-Eyes (Perception + Awareness diff. 7 in the meat to see wraiths) (pp. 477–479).

- **Stains** (pp. 482–484): physical mutations from Angst (Adder's Scales, Armor of Corpulence, Barbed Corpus, Brutish, Chameleon Skin, Compound Eye, Corpus Cilia, Dark Speech, Gossamer Webs, Hammer Fists, Spider's Bristles, Spite-fueled Arcanoi); optional *A Stained Soul* (p. 484) for Wraiths.

## Cross-cutting themes

1. **The divided self.** Every wraith is a Psyche and a Shadow (Ch. 1 p. 21; Ch. 2 pp. 48–49; Ch. 6). Passions feed Pathos and Fetters anchor the wraith; the Shadow feeds on Angst, offers Shadow Dice, triggers Catharsis and sends the wraith into Harrowings (Ch. 6; Ch. 8 pp. 305–311). The same structure returns in the Risen (the Shadow rides the Conduit, Ch. 11), in Spectres (the Psyche becomes the tormentor, Ch. 10), and in other afterlives as hun/p'o (Ch. 13 p. 434), Sattva/Tamas/Rajas (p. 441), the Flayed wraith's dual spirit (p. 452), the African four-part soul (pp. 452–453) and the Creole gros-bon-ange/ti-bon-ange (p. 455). Orpheus projectors have no Shadow and grow Stains instead (pp. 482–484).
2. **Souls as raw material.** Stygia's currency and industry run on souls: obolus, soulforging, soulfire, Skin Artifacts, Harvesters and Thralls (Ch. 2 pp. 42–48; Ch. 12 pp. 424–431). The same horror recurs as White Jade (Ch. 13 pp. 439–440), Svarga's food and soma made from dissolved wraiths (p. 443), and Spectres' bloodfire (p. 428).
3. **Empire, bureaucracy and decay.** The Hierarchy, Legions and Guilds (Ch. 2 pp. 65–93) form a corrupt, aging empire after Charon's disappearance. Jade's four departments (Ch. 13 pp. 435–436), Svarga's caste judges (p. 442) and the Orphic/AEG/Sons of Tertullian orders on the living side (Ch. 12) echo it.
4. **Forbidden contact with the living.** The Dictum Mortuum (Ch. 2 p. 34), the Shroud and Fog (Ch. 8 pp. 282–285), Embody and Puppetry (Ch. 5), the Risen (Ch. 11), mediums, ghost hunters and secret orders (Ch. 12 pp. 406–409) and Orpheus (Appendix) all explore how the dead and living touch and the penalty for it.
5. **Oblivion and the Spectres as mirrors.** Spectres are what wraiths become (Ch. 6 p. 235; Ch. 10); the Labyrinth, Malfeans and the Mouth of the Void (Ch. 2 pp. 25–27; Ch. 10; Appendix p. 490) are the endpoint. Doppelgangers, Nephwracks and Malfeans appear on both sides of the setting; Ch. 10 supplies full rules for playing Spectres.
6. **Transcendence as the one exit** (Ch. 8 pp. 297–300): the Eight Truths and the Tomes of Golden Light; the Far Shores and the Fishers (Ch. 2); and rival ideas of the beyond in Ch. 13 (Peng Lai, Samsara, Ometeotl, Dreamtime).
7. **Storytelling first.** Wraith is "another way to tell ghost stories" (Ch. 1 p. 15): consent and limits (Ch. 1 p. 19; Ch. 4 p. 108; Ch. 11 p. 397 on Target Lock), the Big Three of fear, terror, horror (Ch. 7 p. 258), short chronicles for Spectres and Risen (Ch. 10 p. 370; Ch. 11 pp. 401–403).
8. **Stygia is one dark kingdom among many** (Ch. 13). Alternative cosmologies are given with their own Arcanoi: Jade's Ways (pp. 458–462), Svarga's Displace (pp. 463–464), the Bush of Ghosts' Behest (pp. 465–467).
9. **System design.** One pool mechanic (Attribute + Ability, difficulty 6, a 10 always counts as a success, each 1 cancels a success; Ch. 3 pp. 97–100); Pathos and Angst as mirror resources (Ch. 5 Arcanoi rules, Ch. 6); Common vs. Initiate arts gated by Guild Status (Ch. 5 pp. 146–149); Willpower as the universal safety valve (Ch. 4 p. 115). The optional-rule tone ("if you don't like it, don't use it", p. 95) runs through the rules chapters.
